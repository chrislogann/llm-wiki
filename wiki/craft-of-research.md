# The Craft of Research

**Summary**: A guide to the process of research, detailing how to turn focused topics into questions, find and evaluate sources, construct robust arguments, and write and revise academic reports.

*The Craft of Research* by Wayne Booth, Gregory Colomb, Joseph Williams, Joseph Bizup, and William Fitzgerald is a classic manual on planning, conducting, and writing academic research. It frames research as a dialogic conversation between the researcher and their audience, where arguments are built on reasons and evidence that withstand criticism.

## Key Sections

### Part I: Asking Questions, Seeking Answers
- **Connecting with Your Audience**: Viewing research not as a data dump, but as addressing a problem that the audience cares about.
- **Topics to Questions**: Moving from a broad interest to a focused topic, and then formulating a specific research question.
- **Research Problems**: Defining pure (conceptual) vs. applied research, and articulating the practical or conceptual consequences of a problem ("So what?").

### Part II: Sources and Resources
- **Evaluating Sources**: Categorizing sources into primary, secondary, and tertiary, and assessing their reliability and relevance.
- **Active Engagement**: Reading sources actively for arguments, data, and problems rather than passive summary.
- **Note-Taking**: Managing notes systematically and handling moments of uncertainty during the research phase.

### Part III: Making Your Argument
- **Argument as Conversation**: Framing arguments around five key elements: Claims, Reasons, Evidence, Warrants, and Acknowledgments/Responses.
- **Claims**: Evaluating the type and contestability of claims.
- **Reasons and Evidence**: Distinguishing reasons (sub-claims of logic) from evidence (external data/facts).
- **[[warrants-in-arguments]]**: Explaining and testing the underlying principles that connect reasons to claims.
- **Acknowledgment and Response**: Anticipating reader objections and formulating respectful, logical replies.

### Part IV: Planning, Drafting, and Revising
- **Drafting process**: Developing a plan, writing drafts, and integrating visual data.
- **Revision**: Revising structure, coherence, style, and flow.
