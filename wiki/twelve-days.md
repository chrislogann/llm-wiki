# Twelve Days: The Story of the 1956 Hungarian Revolution

**Summary**: A historical chronicle of the 1956 Hungarian Revolution by Victor Sebestyen, documenting the student protests, [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]]'s reforms, the street fighting, Soviet decisions, and the failure of Western intervention.

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

*Twelve Days: The Story of the 1956 Hungarian Revolution* (published in 2006) by journalist and historian Victor Sebestyen is a detailed chronology of the uprising that shook the Soviet empire. Based on declassified Hungarian and Soviet documents, private diaries, and eyewitness testimony, the book recounts the events of late October and early November 1956 from the streets of Budapest to the high-stakes political decisions in Moscow and Washington (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Key Themes

### 1. The Oppressive Stalinist Backdrop
The revolution was the result of a decade of severe Stalinist oppression under dictator [[matyas-rakosi|Matyas Rakosi]], whose secret police, the [[allamvedelmi-hatosag|AVH]], maintained a climate of fear, executed purges, and forced collectivization (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Khrushchev’s denunciation of Stalin in his 1956 Secret Speech and unrest in Poland catalyzed the public's demands for reform and national independence (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### 2. The Twelve-Day Revolution
Starting as a peaceful student march on October 23, the protest turned violent when the AVH opened fire on crowds outside the Radio Station (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). The people rose in armed rebellion, bringing reformist [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]] back as Prime Minister. For a brief period, the rebels routed initial Soviet forces, and a ceasefire was declared, promising Soviet troop withdrawal (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### 3. Devious Soviet Diplomacy
Soviet Ambassador [[yuri-andropov|Yuri Andropov]] played a central role in deceit, assuring [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] of peaceful negotiations while secretly urging Nikita Khrushchev to deploy the Red Army to crush the "counter-revolution" (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). This experience permanently shaped [[yuri-andropov|Andropov]]'s worldview, initiating a lifelong fear of popular movements (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### 4. Cold War Realpolitik and the Suez Distraction
The revolution coincided with the Suez Crisis, which distracted the United States and divided the Western alliance (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Despite years of bellicose propaganda via Radio Free Europe promising liberation to the "captive nations," the Eisenhower administration decided that Egypt was a higher priority and had no intention of risking nuclear war over Hungary, leaving the Hungarians to fight alone (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### 5. Suppression and Betrayal
On November 4, the Soviet military launched a massive invasion to crush the uprising (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]] was betrayed by his cabinet colleague [[janos-kadar|Janos Kadar]], who formed a pro-Soviet puppet government (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] was arrested, deported, and eventually executed in 1958, while [[janos-kadar|Kadar]] established decades of autocratic rule (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
