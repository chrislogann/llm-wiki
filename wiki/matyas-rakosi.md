# Matyas Rakosi

**Summary**: The Stalinist dictator of Hungary from 1945 to 1956 whose political purges, state terror via the AVH, and forced collectivization generated the intense public grievances that triggered the 1956 Revolution.

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Matyas Rakosi (1892–1971) was a veteran Hungarian Communist who served as a Commissar in Bela Kun’s short-lived Soviet Republic in 1919 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Imprisoned in Hungary for nearly sixteen years under the Horthy regime, he was exchanged to the Soviet Union in 1940, where he became a close sycophant of [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## "Salami Tactics" and Rise to Power
Returning to Hungary with the Red Army in 1945, Rakosi led the KPD (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Despite the Communists receiving only 17% of the vote in the free 1945 election, Rakosi systematically dismantled the democratic opposition (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). He termed this method **[[salami-tactics|salami tactics]]**—slicing away the opposition layer by layer through manufactured plots, arrests of key leaders (like Bela Kovacs), and voter intimidation (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). By 1948, the Communists held untrammeled power, and in 1949, Rakosi established the Hungarian People's Republic (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Dictatorial Rule (1949–1956)
Calling himself "Stalin's best pupil," Rakosi copied the Soviet model in minute detail, establishing one of the most oppressive police states in Eastern Europe (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). His regime was characterized by:
- **State Terror**: The [[allamvedelmi-hatosag|AVH]] secret police arrested over one million Hungarians, torturing and imprisoning hundreds of thousands (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Purges and Show Trials**: He eliminated internal party rivals, most notably executing Foreign Minister Laszlo Rajk in 1949 following a staged trial (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Economic Mismanagement**: He initiated rapid heavy industrialization and forced agricultural collectivization, impoverishing the population and reducing food supplies in a fertile country (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Russification**: Forcing Russian language education, Soviet-style uniforms, and calendar holidays on the populace, which fueled deep national resentment (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Fall and Exile
Following Stalin's death, Rakosi was forced by Moscow to yield the premiership to reformist [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]], though he retained his post as Party First Secretary and continued to sabotage [[imre-nagy|Nagy]]'s reforms (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). After Khrushchev's Secret Speech, public criticism of Rakosi escalated (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). In July 1956, Soviet emissaries removed Rakosi from office, sending him into exile in the Soviet Union (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

Rakosi spent the rest of his life in the USSR, dying in Gorky in 1971 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). His oppressive legacy was the primary catalyst for the [[hungarian-revolution-1956|1956 Revolution]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
