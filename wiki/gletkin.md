# Gletkin

**Summary**: Gletkin is the primary antagonist of *Darkness at Noon*, representing the new generation of Soviet functionaries who use physical deprivation and sleep exhaustion to secure confessions.

**Sources**: `Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Gletkin represents the "New Guard" or the "Neanderthalers" of the totalitarian state (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md). Unlike the older generation of revolutionary intellectuals, Gletkin grew up under the established regime, without any memory of the pre-revolutionary world or the traditions of western democracy (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md).

## Method of Correct Brutality

Gletkin's approach to interrogation is characterized by "correct brutality"—strictly adhering to bureaucratic form while applying intense physical and psychological strain:
- **Sleep Deprivation**: Keeping the prisoner under blinding lights and conducting ceaseless night hearings (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md).
- **Physical Exhaustion**: Gletkin believes that the human nervous system has natural physical limits, and that any prisoner will break once those limits are reached (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md).
- **The "Wax of Centuries"**: Gletkin justifies these methods by arguing that reasoning with uneducated, patriarchal populations (like the Russian peasantry) is impossible. They can only be made to understand or confess after their physical stamina is broken, melting the "wax of centuries" from their ears (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md).

## Conflict with Ivanov

Gletkin's methods contrast sharply with those of his superior, Ivanov, who seeks Rubashov's capitulation through shared logic and comradely persuasion (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md). Gletkin's eventual success in breaking Rubashov leads to Ivanov's arrest and Gletkin's promotion (source: Dialogue_with_Death_9780226449616.md).
