# August Coup (1991)

**Summary**: The abortive August 1991 hardline coup attempt that sought to depose Mikhail Gorbachev, which collapsed in three days and led directly to the breakup of the Soviet Union.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-16

---

The August Coup (August 19–21, 1991) was an abortive attempt by hardline members of the Soviet Union's Communist Party, military, and security services to seize control of the country from President Mikhail Gorbachev. Though the coup collapsed in just three days, it shattered Gorbachev's authority and directly accelerated the dissolution of the Soviet Union.

## The Foros Ultimatum and Gorbachev's House Arrest
On August 18, 1991, hardliners organized under the State Committee on the State of Emergency (SCSE / *GKChP*)—including KGB Chairman Vladimir Kryuchkov, Defense Minister Dmitry Yazov, and Vice President Gennady Yanayev—confronted Gorbachev at his vacation dacha in Foros, Crimea. They issued an ultimatum demanding he sign a state of emergency decree or resign, transferring power to Yanayev (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

Gorbachev refused to sign or resign, calling them criminals. The conspirators cut off his telephone lines, isolated the dacha, and placed him under house arrest guarded by double cordons of KGB forces (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Farce and Disarray of the Plotters
Upon returning to Moscow, the SCSE declared a state of emergency, but their leadership was marked by disarray. Yanayev and Prime Minister Pavlov went on heavy drinking bouts, and Yazov immediately expressed regret. The plotters' televised press conference, where Yanayev's hands visibly shook, turned the coup into a public farce (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## The Moscow Resistance (Led by Yeltsin)
Russian President [[boris-yeltsin|Boris Yeltsin]] immediately denounced the coup as illegal and unconstitutional, calling for a nationwide general strike. From the Russian parliament building (the White House), Yeltsin became the symbol of democratic resistance. Under house arrest, Gorbachev learned of Yeltsin's public defiance via the BBC World Service (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## The Leningrad Resistance (Led by Sobchak)
In Leningrad, military commander General Viktor Samsonov declared a state of emergency and banned protests. St. Petersburg Mayor [[anatoly-sobchak|Anatoly Sobchak]] rushed to Samsonov's headquarters and berated him as a "traitor and executioner," successfully freezing troop deployments. Sobchak then broadcasted live on local TV and rallied 130,000 citizens in Palace Square to support democracy (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

Although the local KGB remained loyal to the state, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], then Sobchak's advisor, resigned from the KGB on August 20 to stand with Sobchak (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Collapse and Aftermath
By August 21, the coup had crumbled. Conspirators including Kryuchkov and Yazov flew back to Foros to plead with Gorbachev, but he ordered them detained. Kryuchkov sent Gorbachev a note expressing deep remorse (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

Gorbachev returned to Moscow on August 22, but his political authority was fatally compromised. He had appointed the hardline coup leaders and failed to prevent the putsch despite foreknowledge (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Yeltsin used the coup's collapse to ban the Communist Party, seize its infrastructure, and lead the Soviet republics toward independence, culminating in the creation of the CIS in December 1991 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
