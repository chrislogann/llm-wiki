# Leon Trotsky

**Summary**: Leon Trotsky (1879-1940) was a leading Marxist theorist and Bolshevik revolutionary who led the Brest-Litovsk negotiations and organized the Red Army, before being exiled and purged by Stalin.

**Sources**: `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`, `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Leon Trotsky was one of the central figures of the Russian Revolution, working alongside Vladimir Lenin to establish the early Soviet state (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## Revolutionary Leadership

Trotsky held key positions during the critical phases of the Soviet state:
- **Commissar for Foreign Affairs**: He headed the negotiations at [[brest-litovsk|Brest-Litovsk]] in 1917-1918, where he implemented the "neither war, nor peace" strategy to broadcast revolutionary ideas to the European working class (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Commissar for Military Affairs**: Following Brest-Litovsk, he built and commanded the Red Army from scratch during the [[russian-civil-war|Russian Civil War]], traveling constantly in a specialized armored train to organize resistance and defeat the White forces (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## Purge and Exile

Following Lenin's death, a bureaucratic faction led by Joseph Stalin seized control of the Communist Party and state apparatus:
- **Purging the Old Guard**: Stalin consolidated power by purging the intellectual "Old Guard" of the revolution (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Fabrication of History**: The Stalinist state systematically rewrote history, removing Trotsky's name and image from historical records, and casting him as the chief conspirator and enemy of the Soviet state (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Exile**: Trotsky was expelled from the Communist Party in 1927, banished to Alma-Ata, and eventually exiled from the USSR in 1929, writing his autobiography in exile (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

This bureaucratic degeneration and the subsequent show trials of Trotskyists are mirrored in the fictional trials of Rubashov in Koestler's *Darkness at Noon* (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
