# Behemoth - the structure and practice of national socialism, 1933-1944

**Summary**: An influential 1942 analysis by Franz Neumann arguing that the Third Reich was not a cohesive, structured state but a chaotic, lawless "monster" driven by competing power centers.

**Sources**: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md, Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

*Behemoth* is considered a classic of modern political analysis, initially published during World War II by Franz Neumann. Its central thesis posits that the Third Reich was characterized by a lack of consistent ideology and coherent structure (source: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md). Like the Behemoth in Jewish mythology or in Thomas Hobbes's writings, Hitler's regime functioned as a chaotic and amorphous monster.

Neumann's title and analysis of the German "non-state" also served as the inspiration for Michael Joseph Roberto's study of the United States interwar period, [[coming-of-the-american-behemoth]], which explores how the "germ of fascism" was nurtured within the highly organized, totalizing American business system (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).

## The Quadrumvirate of Power

Neumann contends that Nazi Germany was essentially a conspiracy among four self-interested, symbiotic but separate power centers:
1. The Nazi Party
2. The German State Bureaucracy
3. The Armed Forces
4. Big Business

These groups continually engaged in turf battles, attempting to expand their power without ceding authority to the others. This led to a system of [[institutional-darwinism]], where governance became a "task state" relying on special plenipotentiaries rather than a strict vertical hierarchy.

For a detailed analysis of how these elites (excluding the Nazi Party itself) survived the Weimar Republic's birth to form this quadrumvirate, see the [[weimar-to-nazism-comparison]] page.

## Ideology and Economics

Applying a [[marxist-analysis]] framework, Neumann viewed Nazi expansionism as driven fundamentally by economic factors (the pursuit of autarky and large-scale capitalism's need for resources). He argued that Nazi ideology was highly opportunistic, lacking a basic political or social theory, instead stealing the ideological clothes of Marxism when convenient (source: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md).

Similarly, he explained Nazi [[anti-semitism]] and actions like Aryanization as maneuvers serving the interests of non-Jewish capitalists or functioning as diversions, rather than recognizing racism as the core driver of Hitler's policies.
