# Nicolas Rubashov

**Summary**: Nicolas Salmanovitch Rubashov is the fictional protagonist of *Darkness at Noon*, an old Bolshevik and ex-Commissar who capitulates and is executed by the totalitarian state he helped create.

**Sources**: `Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Nicolas Salmanovitch Rubashov is a fictional composite representing the "Old Guard" of the Bolshevik Revolution (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md). Formerly a Commissar of the People and division commander in the Revolutionary Army, he is arrested under the orders of "No. 1" on charges of counter-revolutionary activities and planning to poison the leader (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md).

## Moral Compromises and Guilt

Throughout his career in the Party, Rubashov acted strictly according to the principle of "consequent logic," executing policies without moral check:
- **Richard and Little Loewy**: Rubashov sacrificed devoted local party organizers abroad when they diverged from the central strategic line of the Party (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md).
- **Arlova**: His secretary and lover, whom he allowed to be condemned and executed to save his own position and continue his work (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md). The guilt of Arlova's death forms the root of his [[grammatical-fiction|grammatical fiction]].

## Imprisonment and Capitulation

In cell No. 404, Rubashov communicates through cell-wall tapping with No. 402, a tsarist officer whose traditional concepts of honor contrast sharply with Rubashov's utilitarian reason (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md). 
Initially refusing to confess to Gletkin's fabricated charges, Rubashov eventually capitulates after being broken down by sleep deprivation and recognizing that his final service to the Party is to confess publicly, acting as a warning example to the masses (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md). He is executed with a bullet to the back of the neck (source: Darkness_at_Noon_9780226449616.md).
