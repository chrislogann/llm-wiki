# Chinese Eastern Railroad

**Summary**: The Chinese Eastern Railroad (CER) was a strategically vital railway in Manchuria, jointly managed by China and the Soviet Union, which became the flashpoint of the 1929 Sino-Soviet War.

**Sources**: `1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

The Chinese Eastern Railroad (CER), constructed originally during the late Tsarist era, ran across Manchuria, connecting the Trans-Siberian Railway to Vladivostok and Port Arthur (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). Following agreements signed in 1924 (the Peking and Mukden agreements), the railroad was placed under joint Sino-Soviet administration, which explicitly prohibited both states from engaging in propaganda against each other's political or social systems (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

## Seizure and Conflict

Despite the joint agreements, the Soviets maintained dominant control over the railway's departments and utilized its schools, unions, and commercial agencies to disseminate communist propaganda and fund revolutionary activity in China (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). 

Tensions escalated dramatically in 1929:
- **Consulate Raid**: On May 27, 1929, Chinese authorities raided the Soviet consulate in Harbin, seizing documents that they claimed proved Soviet-backed subversion and plots to overthrow the local government (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **The Seizure**: On July 10, 1929, Chinese tupan Lu Yung-huan took unilateral control of the railroad, closing Soviet unions, shutting down pro-Soviet press, and deporting Soviet railway directors (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **The War**: In response to the seizure, the Soviet Union severed diplomatic relations and launched a military offensive (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). 

## Resolution

The military conflict ended in November 1929. The Soviet Special Far Eastern Army (ODVA), utilizing superior air power, artillery, and coordinated cavalry offensives under Vasily Blyukher, routed the Chinese Northeast Frontier Defense Force (NEFDF) (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). The local Chinese administration, led by warlord Chang Hsueh-liang, signed the Khabarovsk Protocol on December 22, 1929, caving to all Soviet demands and restoring the *status quo ante* on the railroad (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
