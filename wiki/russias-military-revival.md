# Russia's Military Revival

**Summary**: Bettina Renz's academic assessment of Russia's post-Soviet military reforms and power projection, detailing the 2008 modernization programme, the role of internal security forces in domestic stability, and a critical deconstruction of the Western-coined concept of "hybrid warfare."

**Sources**: `Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

*Russia's Military Revival* (published in 2018 by Bettina Renz) provides a nuanced analysis of the Kremlin's efforts to rebuild its military capabilities and prestige following the collapse of the Soviet Union (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). Renz argues that Russia's military resurgence is not a return to Cold War expansionism or the invention of a new way of war, but a rational attempt to secure great power status, sovereignty, and regional influence (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md).

## Key Themes

### 1. The Road to Reform: Serdyukov's reforms
After two decades of underfunding, structural obsolescence, and poor performance in conflicts like the **[[russo-georgian-war]]** of 2008, the Kremlin initiated the **[[2008-military-modernization]]** (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). Driven by Defense Minister Anatoly Serdyukov, these "New Look" reforms rationalized the command structure, reduced officer numbers, and introduced a massive State Armament Programme to modernize equipment and transition toward a professionalized, mobile force (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md).

### 2. Deconstructing "Hybrid Warfare"
Renz presents a strong academic critique of the Western label of **[[hybrid-warfare]]** (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). She argues that the 2014 annexation of Crimea was a success because of highly favorable local conditions, rather than a new "Gerasimov Doctrine" or magic war-winning formula (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). In contrast, conflicts in Donbas and Syria demonstrate that Russia continues to rely on conventional military power and traditional grand strategy (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md).

### 3. Russia's "Other" Armed Forces
A crucial component of Russia's security architecture is its **[[internal-security-forces]]**, including the FSB, MVD, and Rosgvardiya (National Guard) (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). Renz highlights that Russia's security focus is inherently inward-looking, with these force structures serving as the primary mechanism for defending the regime against color revolutions and maintaining the **[[vertical-of-power]]** (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md).

### 4. Uses of Military Power Since 1991
The book traces Russian military operations, from 1990s "peacekeeping" in the CIS region and the Kosovo intervention in 1999, to the **[[russo-georgian-war]]** (2008), the annexation of Crimea (2014), and the intervention in the Syrian civil war (2015) (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). Each conflict showed an increasing ability to align military means with political ends, though constrained by economic and demographic limits (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md).
