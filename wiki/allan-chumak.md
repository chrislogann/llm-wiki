# Allan Chumak

**Summary**: A prominent Soviet-era TV psychic, faith healer, and former sports commentator who gained massive popularity during Perestroika by performing silent, energy-charging séances.

**Sources**: `A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md`, `Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-18

---

Allan Chumak was a 54-year-old former Soviet sports commentator who became one of the most famous public figures of the late-Soviet Perestroika era (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md). His televised broadcasts in 1989 drew tens of millions of viewers who sought alternative healing methods during a period of deep economic and healthcare decline (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).

## Healing Methods and Performances
Chumak's televised séances were characterized by complete silence and repetitive physical movements:
* **Silent Gestures**: Chumak sat before the camera soundlessly making repetitive hand gestures, moving his lips without speaking, and occasionally sighing to push back force fields and sweep away bad energy (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **Charging Airwaves**: He claimed to manipulate airwaves through gestures, instructing viewers to place containers of tap water, face creams, or ointments near their TV screens to "charge" them with healing energy (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Non-Specific Philosophy**: Chumak asserted that his spiritual philosophy was communicated to him at age 42 by an "inner voice" (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md). His philosophy was intentionally vague, promising to remedy ailments from allergies to paralysis without committing to concrete clinical mechanisms (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).

## Feud with Kashpirovsky
Chumak stood in stark contrast to his main TV competitor, [[anatoly-kashpirovsky]]:
* **Kindly Mage vs. Demonic Presence**: Chumak was perceived as a gentle, white-haired "white mage," while Kashpirovsky was a menacing, black-clad figure who growled at illnesses and made audience members collapse on stage (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **The Feud**: Although initially friends, the two engaged in a decades-long bitter feud centering around establishing who appeared on television first (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).

## Commercialization and Later Years
* **Monetization**: Chumak capitalized on the public craze by mass-producing pre-charged water, creams, and DVDs, filling entire stadiums without warm-up acts (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **1993 Ban**: In 1993, interest waned as the Ministry of Health banned television psychics for "advocating pseudoscience" (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **Private Practice**: Following the ban, Chumak returned to playing to core fans in smaller venues and charging clinically diagnosed patients hundreds of dollars for private one-to-one healing sessions (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
