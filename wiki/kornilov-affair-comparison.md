# [[kornilov-affair-comparison|The Kornilov Affair: Comparative Analysis]]

**Summary**: A comparative analysis of the accounts of the August 1917 Kornilov Affair in Leon Trotsky's autobiography *My Life* and Antony Beevor's historical work *Russia: Revolution and Civil War*.

**Sources**: `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`, `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **August 1917 [[kornilov-affair-comparison|Kornilov Affair]]** (or Kornilov Mutiny) was a pivotal turning point in the Russian Revolution. By comparing Leon Trotsky’s participant account in [[my-life-trotsky|My Life]] with Antony Beevor’s archival history in [[russia-revolution-and-civil-war|Russia: Revolution and Civil War]], we can contrast a class-conscious political narrative with a detailed analysis of military logistics, miscommunications, and the actions of key individuals.

## Factional Strategy vs. Misunderstanding and Intrigue
The two accounts offer fundamentally different explanations for the outbreak of the crisis:
* **Trotsky's Account (Class and Factional Conflict)**: Trotsky presents the mutiny as a deliberate, class-driven right-wing assault on the revolution, led by General Lavr Kornilov and backed by officers and bourgeoisie (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). In his view, Kerensky and the moderate socialists (Mensheviks and Populists) were powerless to stop it, revealing they had no independent base of support and forcing them to beg the Bolsheviks for defense (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
* **Beevor's Account (Lvov's Mediation and Panic)**: Beevor argues that modern evidence shows Kornilov was not planning a *coup d'état* to overthrow the government, but rather aimed to strengthen it against the Bolshevik threat (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Beevor highlights that the crisis was triggered by Prince V.N. Lvov, a self-appointed mediator whose confused communications led Kerensky to believe Kornilov was demanding supreme power and led Kornilov to believe Kerensky was inviting him to assume a dictatorship (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## The Role of the Bolsheviks and Trotsky's Release
* **Trotsky's Account**: Trotsky recounts his experience inside Kresty prison as the mutiny began. He details how sailors from the cruiser *Aurora* visited him in prison to ask whether they should defend the Winter Palace or assault it, to which he advised them to "put off the squaring of their account with Kerensky until they had finished Kornilov" (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). The government subsequently released Trotsky and other Bolshevik prisoners to organize the defense, which Trotsky utilized to systematically arm the working class (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
* **Beevor's Account**: Beevor confirms that Kerensky was forced to release Trotsky and other Bolshevik leaders, and that the crisis allowed the Bolsheviks to arm the Red Guards and seize control of the Petrograd Soviet's defense committees (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Beevor notes that the affair shattered public trust in parliamentary democracy, rendering Kerensky's government a hollow shell (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## The Resolution: Mass Upsurge vs. Logistical Collapse
* **Trotsky's Account**: Focuses on the political result. Trotsky asserts that the "revolutionary upswing of the masses was so powerful that his mutiny simply melted away and evaporated," functioning as "grist to the Bolshevik mill" and leading directly to the Bolshevik victory in October (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
* **Beevor's Account**: Details the practical mechanics of the collapse. Beevor explains how railway workers sabotaged tracks to halt the troop trains, while Bolshevik agitators infiltrated Kornilov's Cossack divisions to persuade them to lay down their arms (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). He also documents the tragic aftermath, including the suicide of General Krymov and the alienation of the military officer corps, who felt Kerensky had acted in bad faith and betrayed Kornilov (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## Comparative Matrix

| Theme | *My Life* (Trotsky) | *Russia: Revolution and Civil War* (Beevor) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Outbreak Cause** | Class-driven, planned counter-revolutionary coup by reactionary forces | Disastrous chain of misunderstandings engineered by V.N. Lvov |
| **Kornilov's Intent** | To crush the revolution and slaughter the Bolsheviks | To strengthen the government and restore military discipline |
| **Bolshevik Role** | The decisive defense force; strategically arming the workers | Exploited the panic to arm Red Guards and gain legitimacy |
| **Kerensky's Action** | Begged the Bolsheviks for help; exposed the weakness of his regime | Panicked, denounced Kornilov, and alienated the officer corps |
| **The Warlords' Response** | (Not mentioned in Trotsky's personal Petrograd narrative) | Warlords like Wrangel and Mannerheim clashed over supporting the coup |
