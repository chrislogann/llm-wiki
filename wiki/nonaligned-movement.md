# Nonaligned Movement

**Summary**: A political initiative formed by developing nations to remain independent of the United States and Soviet Union power blocs, emerging from the 1955 Bandung Conference to assert a distinct "Third World" path.

**Sources**: `Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The Nonaligned Movement (NAM) was established as an international forum for former colonial or semicolonial countries seeking to maintain sovereignty and resist superpower domination during the Cold War (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Origins: The Bandung Conference
In April 1955, leaders from twenty-nine Asian and African nations met in Bandung, Indonesia, for the first major postcolonial summit (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Prominent figures such as Sukarno of Indonesia, Jawaharlal Nehru of India, Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, and Marshal **[[josip-broz-tito]]** of Yugoslavia sought to define a collective political stance separate from both Western capitalism and Soviet communism (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## The Third World Project
The Bandung Conference popularized the term "Third World," which carried connotations of the French "third estate"—the global majority that had been oppressed by colonialism but was now claiming its political voice (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). The movement championed:
- Mutual respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
- De-escalation of superpower tensions and disarmament (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
- Resistance to foreign military bases and interventions (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Vulnerability to Superpower Influence
Despite their aspirations for nonalignment, many Third World governments were weak, economically underdeveloped, and beset by regional rivalries (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). As a result, local elites often broke with the nonaligned ideal and entered into alliances with the United States or Soviet Union to secure arms and development aid, drawing their countries directly into the global Cold War conflict (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
