# Communicating with Intelligence

**Summary**: A guide to written and oral communication in the intelligence community, detailing the requirements of analytical writing, structured argumentation, and briefings for decision makers.

*Communicating with Intelligence* by M. Patrick Hendrix and James S. Major focuses on the specialized skills required to turn raw information into finished intelligence and effectively communicate it to policy makers and decision makers. The book establishes a clear distinction between academic research and intelligence analysis, focusing on action-oriented writing and briefings.

## Key Sections

### Part I: The Foundations of Good Intelligence Communication
- **The Analyst's Voice**: Identifies analysts as the core voice of the intelligence community who translate raw data into actionable insights.
- **Audience Focus**: Emphasizes the need to tailor products to busy, generalist decision makers who require clarity, brevity, and relevance.
- **The Core Goals**: Establishes the [[three-goals-of-intelligence-writing]]: to judge, to interpret, and to support.
- **Analytical Argument**: Focuses on structured reasoning, thesis formulation, and refuting counterarguments in an intelligence context.

### Part II: Writing with Intelligence
- **Research Methods**: Discusses time management, open-source intelligence collection, and evaluating source credibility (originator, date, publisher).
- **Structured Analysis**: Introduces structured analytic techniques (SATs) and prewriting strategies.
- **Drafting and Revising**: Focuses on structuring analytical papers, starting with the [[bottom-line-up-front]] (BLUF), crafting key judgments, and applying top-down/bottom-up revision methods.

### Part III: Briefing with Intelligence
- **Scripting and Organization**: Details the typologies of briefings, the use of visual aids, and the ABCs of good briefers.
- **Customer Interaction**: Focuses on appearance, demeanor, and presenting unwelcome findings to senior policy makers.

### Part IV: Citations and Classified Materials
- **Intelink and Open Source**: Outlines proper citation guidelines for unclassified and classified sources, formatting bibliography records, and the rules surrounding classified information handling.

## Historic Intelligence Examples Included
- **Operation Neptune Spear (2011)**: Tracking Osama bin Laden to the Abbottabad compound using satellites and safe-house surveillance, highlighting the challenges of probabilistic analysis (estimates ranging from 40% to 95% confidence).
- **Able Archer 83**: A special National Intelligence Estimate (SNIE) assessing Soviet fears of a surprise nuclear attack during NATO military exercises.
- **Yugoslavia Transformed (NIE 15-90)**: A prescient 1990 intelligence estimate forecasting the disintegration of Yugoslavia within one year and the ensuing violent intercommunal conflict.
- **Appendix A Memo**: Herbert E. Meyer's 1983 memo, [[why-is-the-world-so-dangerous-memo]], discussing late-Cold War geopolitics.
