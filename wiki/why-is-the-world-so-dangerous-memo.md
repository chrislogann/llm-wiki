# Why Is the World So Dangerous Memo

**Summary**: A landmark November 30, 1983 memorandum by National Intelligence Council Vice Chairman Herbert E. Meyer analyzing rising global violence as a Soviet counter-strategy to offset a shifting "correlation of forces" in favor of the Free World.

**Sources**: [Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md](file:///home/chrislogann/Desktop/repository/llm-wiki/raw/Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

The **"Why Is the World So Dangerous?"** memorandum was submitted by Herbert E. Meyer, Vice Chairman of the National Intelligence Council, to the Director of Central Intelligence (DCI) and Deputy Director of Central Intelligence (DDCI) on November 30, 1983 (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).

The memo addressed a sudden surge in global violence during late 1983, including:
- The shoot-down of KAL Flight 007 by Soviet interceptors.
- The assassination of Benigno Aquino in the Philippines.
- The Rangoon bombing targeting South Korea's leadership.
- The terrorist bombings of US, French, and Israeli soldiers in Lebanon.
- The Libyan invasion of Chad.
- The Grenada coup that triggered the US invasion.

## The Shifting "Correlation of Forces"

Meyer argued that the outbreak of violence was not coincidental but rather a coordinated or teleguided Soviet effort to fight back against a reversing global balance of power. He contrasted the Soviet outlook of 1979 against 1983:

### 1979 Soviet Outlook (Favorable)
- Stagnant US economy and low US defense spending.
- Steady flow of wealth and Western technology from West to East.
- Successful spread of Soviet influence in the Third World via surrogates (e.g., Cuba, Vietnam).
- Shift in European balance of power via deployment of Soviet SS-20 missiles.

### 1983 Soviet Outlook (Unfavorable)
- **Economic Recovery**: Recovering US economy and rising US defense spending.
- **Economic Deficits**: Crudely reduced flow of wealth and technology, highlighted by US opposition cutting the Siberia-to-Western Europe pipeline project in half.
- **Third World Resistance**: Emerging anti-Soviet insurgencies in Nicaragua, Mozambique, Angola, Kampuchea, and Afghanistan (directly countering the [[reagan-doctrine]]).
- **NATO Response**: Certain deployment of Pershing II and cruise missiles in Europe.
- **Truth as a Weapon**: President Reagan describing the Soviet Union as the "focus of evil."

## Predictions of Soviet Collapse

Meyer asserted that if the Soviet Union did not displace the US within approximately 20 years, it would fail completely. He highlighted three fatal structural vulnerabilities:
1. **Empire, Not a Country**: The USSR remained a 19th-century-style empire containing over 100 nationality groups seeking political and economic freedom from Russian control.
2. **Economic Stagnation**: A demographic and arithmetic calamity where Soviet GDP growth was less than 2% while military spending increased at 4% annually.
3. **Demographic Shift**: Only half the population spoke Russian, birthrates among Russians were declining (shrinking the industrial labor force), and high birthrates in Moslem republics drained capital investment.

## The Soviet Strategy of Violence

To knock the US off its course, Meyer argued the Soviets adopted a strategy of raising global violence to:
1. Attribute the dangerous international climate to "reckless" US policies.
2. Rely on Western media and elites to amplify this narrative.
3. Induce Western voters to replace incumbent leaders or force a policy retreat.

Meyer warned that as the Soviet Union descended into history, the Cold War would enter its most dangerous phase, transforming into a "bare knuckled street fight" (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).
