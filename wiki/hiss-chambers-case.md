# [[alger-hiss|Hiss]]-[[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] Case

**Summary**: One of the most famous legal and political cases of the early Cold War, in which former Communist courier [[whittaker-chambers|Whittaker Chambers]] accused former State Department official [[alger-hiss|Alger Hiss]] of Soviet espionage, exposing deep Soviet infiltration in the US government.

**Sources**: `Witness_9780895267894.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **[[alger-hiss|Hiss]]-[[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] Case** began on August 3, 1948, when senior *Time* editor [[whittaker-chambers|Whittaker Chambers]] testified before the House Un-American Activities Committee (HUAC), naming [[alger-hiss|Alger Hiss]] as a member of a secret Communist underground in Washington D.C. in the 1930s (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## HUAC Hearings and the Libel Suit
[[alger-hiss|Hiss]], who had recently resigned from the State Department to head the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, requested a hearing and denied the charges under oath (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). He claimed he had never met [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

A HUAC subcommittee, spearheaded by Congressman **Richard Nixon**, set up a series of confrontations between the two men (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Nixon noted that [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] possessed intimate knowledge of [[alger-hiss|Hiss]]'s daily life, habits, and homes (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Under pressure, [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] admitted to having known [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] under the pseudonym "George Crosley" in 1934, but denied he was a Communist (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

After [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] repeated his charges on the radio show *Meet the Press*, [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] filed a $75,000 libel suit against him (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## The "Pumpkin Papers"
To defend himself in the libel suit, [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] retrieved his "life preserver" files (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). In November 1948, he produced 65 typed pages of secret State Department documents and hand-written summaries by [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] and [[harry-dexter-white|Harry Dexter White]] (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

In December 1948, fearing his home would be searched, [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] hid five rolls of developed and undeveloped microfilm containing top-secret government documents inside a hollowed-out pumpkin on his Maryland farm (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). The microfilm, which became famously known as the **"Pumpkin Papers,"** proved that [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] had systematically stolen secret State Department reports (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Perjury Trials
Because the ten-year statute of limitations for espionage had expired, [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] was indicted by a federal grand jury on two counts of perjury (lying about passing documents to [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] and lying about meeting him after January 1938) (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 
- **First Trial (1949)**: Ended in a hung jury (8–4 for conviction) (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).
- **Second Trial (1950)**: On January 21, 1950, [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] was found guilty on both counts and sentenced to five years at Lewisburg Federal Penitentiary (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Geopolitical and Cultural Impact
The case deeply polarized American society. For the American liberal establishment, [[alger-hiss|Hiss]] was seen as an innocent victim of a conservative "witch hunt," while for conservatives, [[alger-hiss|Hiss]]'s conviction validated fears of deep Communist subversion in the federal government (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). The case propelled Richard Nixon to national prominence and helped launch the era of McCarthyism (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
