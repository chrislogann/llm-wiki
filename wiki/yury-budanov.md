# Yury Budanov

**Summary**: A Russian Army colonel convicted of the abduction, rape, and murder of a young Chechen woman, whose trial became a symbol of military impunity and human rights struggles in [[chechnya|Chechnya]].

**Sources**: `[[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md`, `Chechnya_9780330350754.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Colonel Yury Budanov (1963–2011) was the commander of the 160th Tank Regiment during the [[second-chechen-war|Second Chechen War]] (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). He became a highly polarizing figure in Russia after he was arrested for war crimes committed against Chechen civilians (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## The Crime
On March 26, 2000—the day [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] was first elected president—Budanov entered the village of Tangi-Chu with his soldiers, abducted eighteen-year-old Elza Kungaeva, and took her to his quarters (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). He interrogated, raped, and strangled her to death, ordering his subordinates to bury her body in a secret grave (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## The Trial and Military Impunity
Budanov’s arrest was a rare exception in [[chechnya|Chechnya]], where security forces routinely committed crimes with total impunity (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). His trial in Rostov-on-Don became a major political battleground:
- **Military Defense**: The Russian military establishment and nationalist politicians defended Budanov, claiming he was a hero who had killed a suspected Chechen sniper (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). General Vladimir Shamanov and other officers openly supported him in the courtroom (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).
- **Psychiatric Controversy**: Pro-Kremlin state medical boards repeatedly found Budanov to be "temporarily insane" during the crime in an attempt to have him acquitted and released (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).
- **International Pressure**: The trial only shifted toward accountability after the Memorial Civil Rights Center and international psychiatrists exposed the bias of the Russian reports, prompting European leaders (including Germany's Gerhard Schröder) to pressure [[vladimir-putin|Putin]] (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## Conviction and Assassination
In July 2003, Budanov was found guilty, stripped of his rank and military honors, and sentenced to ten years in a strict-regime labor camp (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). 

Budanov was released on parole in January 2009, sparking widespread protests in [[chechnya|Chechnya]] (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). In June 2011, he was shot and killed in Moscow by an unidentified gunman, an act widely viewed as a revenge assassination (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).
