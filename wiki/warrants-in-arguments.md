# Warrants in Arguments

**Summary**: The logical principle that licenses an inference, connecting a specific reason to a specific claim by establishing that a general circumstance predictably implies a general consequence.

In academic and professional research, a **Warrant** is the underlying principle that connects a reason to a claim (source: Craft_of_Research_9780226826660.md). When a reader accepts that a reason is true but asks *"So what? How does that follow?"*, they are questioning the relevance of the reason to the claim. The warrant answers this by justifying the logical link.

## Structure of a Warrant

A warrant is structured in a generalized form:
> **General Circumstance (X) $\rightarrow$ General Consequence (Y)**

When we apply a warrant to a specific argument, we verify that:
1. The specific **Reason** is a valid instance of the **General Circumstance (X)**.
2. The specific **Claim** is a valid instance of the **General Consequence (Y)**.

```
       [Warrant]  If General Circumstance (X) occurs ---> then General Consequence (Y) follows.
                           |                                           |
                   (is an instance of)                         (is an instance of)
                           |                                           |
                           v                                           v
         [Reason]  Specific Circumstance (x) ---------> [Claim] Specific Consequence (y).
```

### Everyday Example
- **Reason**: The FBI has been questioning the mayor's staff.
- **Claim**: The mayor must be involved in something crooked.
- **Warrant (Proverb)**: Where there is smoke (signs of crooked behavior), there is fire (crooked behavior in fact exists) (source: Craft_of_Research_9780226826660.md).

### Specialized Research Example
- **Reason**: Japan's fertility rate is only 1.3 and falling.
- **Claim**: Japan faces a declining standard of living.
- **Warrant**: When a nation's labor force shrinks (General Circumstance), its economic future is grim (General Consequence) (source: Craft_of_Research_9780226826660.md).

## Characteristics of Warrants in Research

- **Disciplinary Context**: Warrants in specialized fields are often not common sense but specialized principles (e.g., how biologists use DNA similarity to measure relationships, or how historians use wills to estimate asset ownership). Learning to think like a professional in a field largely involves learning its accepted warrants (source: Craft_of_Research_9780226826660.md).
- **Implicit vs. Explicit**: Experts rarely state warrants explicitly when writing for other experts within their own field, as doing so might seem condescending. However, they must state them when writing for broader audiences or when their core assumptions are challenged.
- **Compressed Warrants**: Warrants are often compressed into short assertions (e.g., *"Shared DNA is the measure of the relationship between species"* instead of *"When species share more DNA, they are more closely related"*). For clarity, they are best analyzed in their explicit *"When X, then Y"* form.
