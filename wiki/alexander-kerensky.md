# [[alexander-kerensky|Alexander Kerensky]]

**Summary**: Alexander Fyodorovich Kerensky (1881–1970) was a prominent Russian politician who served as the Minister-President of the Russian Provisional Government in 1917, attempting to sustain Russia's war effort and democratic reforms before being overthrown by the Bolsheviks.

**Sources**: `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`, `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

**[[alexander-kerensky|Alexander Kerensky]]** was the central political figure of the Russian Provisional Government between the February and October Revolutions of 1917 (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). His administration attempted to navigate a moderate path between the conservative military Right and the radical socialist Left, but ultimately alienated both, paving the way for the Bolshevik seizure of power (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## Factional Conflicts and the Bolsheviks
In his autobiography *My Life*, [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]] characterizes Kerensky as a "petty braggart" and an adventitious, histrionic figure (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md):
- **Slander and July Days**: Following the July Days of 1917, Kerensky’s government actively persecuted the Bolsheviks, accusing Lenin and Trotsky of being paid German spies (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). Trotsky was arrested and imprisoned in Kresty, while Lenin went into hiding in Finland (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Soviet Maneuvers**: Trotsky details how he used Kerensky's immense unpopularity to win over the Petrograd Soviet, famously linking the moderate Soviet presidium directly to Kerensky's failed policies to shift the delegates' support to the Bolsheviks (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## The Kornilov Crisis and Decline
Both sources highlight the **[[kornilov-affair-comparison|Kornilov Affair]]** in August 1917 as the fatal blow to Kerensky's authority:
* **Distrust and Miscommunication**: Antony Beevor notes that Kerensky was plagued by growing paranoia, a persecution complex, and declining power (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Aggrieved by the right-wing press's support for General Lavr Kornilov, Kerensky fell victim to muddled reports from Prince V.N. Lvov, leading him to panic and denounce Kornilov as a traitor (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
* **Arming the Red Guards**: To defend Petrograd against Kornilov's cavalry, Kerensky was forced to release Trotsky and other jailed Bolshevik leaders and arm the working-class Red Guards (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md, My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). Trotsky remarked that this crisis proved "Kerensky and Co. revealed the fact that they had no forces of their own to back them" (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
* **Alienation of the Officers**: Beevor details how Kerensky's betrayal of Kornilov disgusted senior military figures (such as General Alekseev, who resigned in protest), leaving Kerensky's government completely isolated and undefended when the Bolsheviks launched the October Revolution (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
