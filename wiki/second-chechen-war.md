# [[second-chechen-war|Second Chechen War]]

**Summary**: The military campaign launched by Russia in 1999 under Prime Minister [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], resulting in the re-establishment of federal control over [[chechnya|Chechnya]] and playing a pivotal role in Putin's political ascent.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`, `Chechnya_9780330350754.md`, `Putin's_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The [[second-chechen-war|Second Chechen War]] (1999–2009) began in the autumn of 1999, breaking three years of fragile peace established by the Khasavyurt Accord that ended the [[first-chechen-war|First Chechen War]] (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). It was launched in response to two key events: the armed incursion of Chechen extremists led by [[shamil-basayev|Shamil Basayev]] into Dagestan, and a series of apartment bombings in Russian cities that killed nearly 300 civilians and were officially blamed on Chechen terrorists (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Course of the Conflict

### The Invasion and "Outhouse" Rhetoric
Appointed Prime Minister shortly before the conflict, [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] oversaw the military response. He captured the public's imagination with tough, uncompromising language, famously declaring: "We will pursue the terrorists everywhere... if we catch them in the outhouse, we will waste them in the outhouse" (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Russian forces launched a massive aerial and ground campaign, blockading Grozny and advancing methodically to minimize Russian casualties, unlike the reckless assault of 1994 (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

### The Siege of Grozny (1999–2000)
Grozny was subjected to an intense siege. Russian forces utilized thermobaric weapons and heavy artillery, virtually flattening the city (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). In February 2000, Chechen fighters attempted a breakout through minefields, suffering massive losses, and the city fell back under Russian control (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

### Guerrilla Phase and Counter-Insurgency
Following the capture of Grozny, the conflict shifted to a long-running guerrilla campaign in the southern mountains. Under Putin, Russia employed a strategy of **"Chechenization"**—co-opting former rebel commanders, most notably Akhmad Kadyrov (the Chief Mufti of [[chechnya|Chechnya]]) and his son Ramzan Kadyrov, to govern the republic and combat the remaining insurgents (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Human Rights Violations and Impunity
The war was characterized by widespread war crimes committed by Russian federal forces against civilians (source: Putin's_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). These crimes were shielded by a system of [[military-impunity-in-russia|military impunity]], where military prosecutors routinely blocked investigations (source: Putin's_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). High-profile exceptions, such as the rape and murder of Elza Kungaeva by Colonel [[yury-budanov|Yury Budanov]], only came to trial due to international pressure, while other atrocities like the GRU's Dai massacre (the execution of six civilians) went unpunished (source: Putin's_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## Terrorism and Escalation
Faced with defeat on the ground, radical factions led by [[shamil-basayev|Shamil Basayev]] launched massive terrorist attacks inside Russia, including the 2002 [[dubrovka-theater-siege|Dubrovka Theater hostage crisis]] and the 2004 Beslan school siege (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). These attacks resulted in hundreds of civilian deaths and were used by Putin to justify further centralization of political control in Russia (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Conclusion and Legacy
Russia officially declared the "counter-terrorist operation" in [[chechnya|Chechnya]] completed in April 2009. The war succeeded in reintegrating [[chechnya|Chechnya]] into the Russian Federation under the autocratic rule of the Kadyrov clan, but at the cost of widespread human rights abuses and the wholesale destruction of the region's infrastructure (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
