# Salami Tactics

**Summary**: A political strategy characterized by the systematic, incremental elimination of political opposition "slice-by-slice," famously used by the Soviet Union to establish satellite states in postwar Eastern Europe.

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

**"Salami Tactics"** (*szeletelő taktika* in Hungarian, also known as *Rákosi-Taktik*) describes a strategy of political takeover where an opposition or democratic system is dismantled incrementally rather than in a single coup (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

The term was coined by Hungarian Communist leader [[matyas-rakosi|Mátyás Rákosi]] to describe the deceptive, step-by-step methodology the Communist Party used to achieve absolute control over Hungary between 1945 and 1948, despite initial electoral defeat (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Core Progression of the Strategy

### 1. Moderate Coalitions
Initially, the Communist Party presented a moderate, non-threatening platform, participating in broad anti-fascist coalition governments alongside democratic parties (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). This was designed to appease Western allies and avoid alarming the domestic electorate (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

### 2. Securing the Security Services
The Communists prioritized taking absolute control over the Ministry of the Interior, the police, and the domestic intelligence services, such as the Hungarian [[allamvedelmi-hatosag|AVH]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). This gave them the physical monopoly on state force (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### 3. Slicing the Opposition
Once they controlled the security apparatus, they used manufactured conspiracies, blackmail, and targeted arrests to split, discredit, and slice away the right-wing and moderate sections of the opposition parties (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). In Hungary, they forced dozens of democratic Smallholders Party MPs to resign under false accusations of treason (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### 4. Co-opting and Merging the Left
Finally, the Communists targeted the remaining center-left parties, such as the Social Democrats, forcing them into a political merger to create a single-party state under Communist leadership (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Historical Impact
Salami tactics became the blueprint for the Soviet Union’s postwar [[soviet-satellization|satellization]] of Eastern Europe (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). By implementing these incremental methods, Moscow established subservient puppet regimes in Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, and Czechoslovakia (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). The intense public grievances built up during the subsequent years of dictatorial rule eventually culminated in the [[hungarian-revolution-1956|Hungarian Revolution of 1956]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
