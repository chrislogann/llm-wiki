# Katyn Massacre

**Summary**: The mass execution in April–May 1940 of nearly 22,000 Polish military officers, police, and intellectuals by the Soviet NKVD under orders signed by Joseph Stalin and the Politburo, followed by a fifty-year Soviet state disinformation campaign blaming Nazi Germany.

**Sources**: `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The Katyn Massacre was a state-directed mass murder conducted by the Soviet NKVD in spring 1940 following the joint Nazi-Soviet invasion and partition of Poland under the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). The executions were designed to eliminate the military, intellectual, and political leadership of the Polish nation to prevent any future resurgence of an independent Polish state (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Archival Evidence and Execution Order

The primary archival documentation for the massacre was preserved in the top-secret "Package No. 1" of the CPSU Central Committee (Fond 89) (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md):
- **Beria's Proposal (March 5, 1940)**: NKVD chief Lavrentiy Beria submitted a formal memorandum (Document No. 794/B) arguing that Polish officers and prisoners of war held in Kozelsk, Starobelsk, and Ostashkov camps were "hardened, uncompromising enemies of Soviet power" and recommended they be tried before NKVD *troikas* and subjected to the "supreme punishment: execution by shooting" (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **Politburo Signatures**: The proposal was personally approved and countersigned in blue pencil by Joseph Stalin, Vyacheslav Molotov, Kliment Voroshilov, and Anastas Mikoyan, with recorded affirmative votes from Mikhail Kalinin and Lazar Kaganovich (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **The Toll**: A total of 21,857 Polish citizens—including over 4,400 at Katyn Forest near Smolensk, 3,800 at Starobelsk (buried near Kharkov), 6,300 at Ostashkov (buried at Mednoye), and 7,300 in western Ukrainian and Belarusian prisons—were shot and buried in mass graves (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## The Disinformation Campaign and Historical Revelation

1. **Discovery and the Burdenko Commission (1943–1944)**:
   - In April 1943, the German army uncovered mass graves in the Katyn Forest and invited an International Medical Commission and the Polish Red Cross to investigate (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - When the Polish government-in-exile under General Władysław Sikorski requested an independent International Red Cross inquiry, Stalin severed diplomatic relations with the London Poles (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - After recapturing the Smolensk region, Moscow established the "Special Commission for the Establishment and Investigation of the Circumstances of the Shooting of Polish Prisoners of War by German-Fascist Invaders" (the Burdenko Commission), which fabricated forensic evidence and witness testimonies to attribute the crime to the German Wehrmacht in autumn 1941 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

2. **Western Complicity and Soviet Denial**:
   - For geopolitical reasons, the Roosevelt and Churchill administrations actively discouraged public investigations of Katyn to preserve the wartime alliance with the Soviet Union (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - Soviet attempts to include Katyn in the Nuremberg Trials indictment in 1946 collapsed when Soviet prosecutors failed to present credible evidence against German defendants (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

3. **Declassification and Admission (1990–1992)**:
   - On April 13, 1990, Mikhail Gorbachev officially expressed deep regret, admitting that the executions were carried out by the NKVD and represented "one of the grave crimes of Stalinism" (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - In October 1992, Russian President Boris Yeltsin transmitted the original signed Politburo execution orders from Package No. 1 to Polish President Lech Wałęsa, definitively ending fifty years of official falsehood (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
