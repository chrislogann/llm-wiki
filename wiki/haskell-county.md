# Haskell County, Kansas

**Summary**: A flat, specialized wheat-farming county in southwestern Kansas that suffered extreme agricultural instability during the Dust Bowl and became deeply dependent on New Deal federal assistance.

**Sources**: [Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Haskell County, Kansas, centered around the town of Sublette, served as a key case study in Donald Worster's analysis of the [[dust-bowl|Dust Bowl]] (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). 

## Wheat Gambling and Soil Instability
Unlike Cimarron County, which had a mix of ranching and farming, Haskell County by the 1930s was almost exclusively dedicated to specialized wheat farming (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). Sociologist Earl Bell, studying the county for the Bureau of Agricultural Economics, observed that Haskell's farmers behaved as economic gamblers, driven by a maximizing ethos to risk all for a bumper crop (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). 

## Dependence on the New Deal
While Haskell County suffered slightly less wind erosion and population flight than Cimarron County, its monoculture farming left it highly vulnerable to crop failures (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). To survive the consecutive years of drought and crop failure, the county became heavily dependent on the federal government's New Deal relief programs, illustrating how the capitalist farming model relied on the welfare state to absorb its environmental risks (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md).
