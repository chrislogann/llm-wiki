# Good vs. Bad Capitalism

**Summary**: The ideological false dichotomy in American politics that distinguishes between productive "good" capitalism and predatory "bad" monopoly capital, obscuring the structural links between liberalism, the New Deal, and state monopoly capitalism.

**Sources**: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **false dichotomy of "good vs. bad" capitalism** is a persistent feature of American political discourse, prominently exemplified by President Franklin D. Roosevelt's rhetoric and policies during the late New Deal period (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).

## Historical Context

- **The Roosevelt Recession (1937–1938)**: Following budget cuts and a reduction in deficit spending in 1937, the United States plunged into a sudden and severe recession, raising unemployment and instability. This crisis forced Roosevelt to confront the structural limits of New Deal recovery policies, which had been driven primarily by consumption rather than capital investment (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The 1938 Monopoly Inquiry**: In April 1938, facing business opposition and the threat of an authoritarian backlash, Roosevelt requested Congress to initiate an inquiry into the concentration of economic power (which led to the Temporary National Economic Committee, or TNEC). In his address, Roosevelt defined fascism as the ownership of government by a controlling private power and warned that the growing concentration of monopoly power was a direct threat to democracy (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **Good vs. Bad Capitalism**: Roosevelt's rhetoric framed the crisis around a moralistic division within the capitalist class: the "overwhelming majority" of businessmen who intended to be "good citizens," versus a "condemned minority" of monopolists, financial manipulators, and price-riggers. By blaming the crisis on the abuses of a predatory minority, Roosevelt preserved the legitimacy of the capitalist system as a whole, presenting state intervention as a defense of "good" capitalism (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **Liberalism and State Corporatism**: Critics of the New Deal from the left pointed out the structural connection between liberalism and fascism during the transition to state monopoly capitalism. The National Recovery Administration (NRA) codes, which allowed trade associations to self-govern and set prices, resembled the corporative systems of fascist Italy and Germany, demonstrating how liberal reform could act as a transition to fascist-style class control under the business system (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
