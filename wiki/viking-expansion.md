# Viking Expansion

**Summary**: The historical period of Scandinavian naval expansion between 700 and 1000 AD, which was falsely portrayed by Nazi propaganda as a unified, predestined empire to justify WWII expansion.

**Sources**: `Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Historically, the Viking Age (roughly 700 to 1000 AD) was marked by significant naval pioneering, raiding, and colonization by Scandinavian peoples (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).

## Historical Reality vs. Nazi Propaganda

Nazi propaganda posters, such as "Nordmannafolket" and recruitment advertisements for the Waffen-SS, compared Nazi expansion in WWII to the voyages of Viking ancestors (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). They urged volunteers in occupied Norway to go east to fight Bolshevism, framing the war as a predestined expansion akin to a "Manifest Destiny" (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). 

However, this comparison relied on several historical inaccuracies:
1. **Decentralization**: Early Viking communities were independently ruled by local chieftains, not a unified national empire (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). Even as larger kingdoms arose in Denmark, Sweden, and Norway, regional conflicts were common and nationalist sentiments were low (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
2. **Freedom from Oppression**: Many Viking colonies (e.g., Iceland) were established by people seeking freedom from domestic political and religious oppression rather than expanding an empire (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
3. **Exclusion of Germans**: The land-locked Germanic tribes of the Viking era did not participate in the naval expansion (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). A natural buffer zone of infertile soils in southern Jutland separated the continental Germans from the Scandinavians (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). The raiders and settlers originated almost exclusively from what is now Denmark, Norway, Iceland, and Sweden (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
