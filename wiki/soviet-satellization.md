# Soviet Satellization

**Summary**: The geopolitical and political process (1945–1948) by which the Soviet Union converted the nations of Central and Eastern Europe into dependent communist satellite states through military occupation, coercive coalitions, and "salami tactics."

**Sources**: `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Following the defeat of Nazi Germany in 1945, the Soviet Union established direct hegemony over the territories liberated or occupied by the Red Army across Central and Eastern Europe (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Through a combination of military intimidation, secret police infiltration, and staged political transitions, Moscow installed subservient communist regimes in Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, and East Germany (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Mechanisms of Satellization

1. **"Salami Tactics" (*Rákosi-Taktik*)**:
   - Coined by Hungarian communist leader Mátyás Rákosi, this method involved the systematic, incremental elimination of non-communist opposition parties "slice by slice" (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - Communists initially participated in broad anti-fascist national front coalitions, took control of the Ministry of the Interior and security services, and used manufactured conspiracies and intimidation to discredit and ban rival democratic parties (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

2. **The Polish Model**:
   - Despite British and American wartime insistence on free Polish elections, the Soviets imposed the pro-Moscow Lublin Committee (PKWN) and systematically arrested the non-communist Polish underground (Home Army) leadership (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - The memory of the 1940 [[katyn-massacre|Katyn Massacre]] and the Soviet refusal to assist the 1944 Warsaw Uprising underscored the ruthless consolidation of control, culminating in falsified elections in January 1947 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

3. **The Czechoslovak Coup (February 1948)**:
   - Czechoslovakia remained a genuine multi-party democracy until February 1948, when Klement Gottwald's Communist Party mobilized armed workers' militias and police forces to force President Edvard Beneš into accepting an all-communist government (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - The suspicious death of non-communist Foreign Minister Jan Masaryk symbolized the total absorption of the country into the Soviet bloc (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Resistance and Crises in the Satellite Bloc

Soviet hegemony was repeatedly contested by popular insurrections and intra-bloc schisms (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md):
- **Tito-Stalin Split (1948)**: Josip Broz Tito's refusal to subordinate Yugoslavia to Moscow led to Yugoslavia's expulsion from the Cominform and multiple Soviet assassination plots against Tito (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **East Berlin Uprising (June 1953)**: East German workers revolted against increased work quotas; the rebellion was crushed by Soviet tanks (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **Hungarian Revolution (October–November 1956)**: Imre Nagy declared Hungarian neutrality and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact; the Soviet military invaded Budapest, deposed Nagy (who was later executed), and restored hardline control (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **Prague Spring and Brezhnev Doctrine (1968)**: Alexander Dubček's attempt to build "Socialism with a human face" was terminated by the Warsaw Pact invasion in August 1968, institutionalizing the doctrine that Moscow retained the right to intervene militarily in any socialist state where party rule was threatened (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **The 1989 Revolutions**: When Mikhail Gorbachev rescinded the Brezhnev Doctrine, the satellite regimes collapsed rapidly between August and December 1989 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
