# My Life - An Attempt at an Autobiography

**Summary**: A summary of [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]]'s autobiography, detailing his role in the Russian Revolution, the Brest-Litovsk peace negotiations, the creation of the Red Army, and his subsequent exile and purge by Stalin.

**Sources**: `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-22

---

[[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]]'s *My Life* offers a first-hand account of the major events of the Russian revolutionary movement, the rise and victory of the Bolsheviks, and the subsequent political struggles inside the early Soviet state (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## Key Themes

- **Brest-Litovsk Negotiations**: Details his role as Commissar for Foreign Affairs, conducting negotiations with the Central Powers, implementing the "neither war, nor peace" strategy, and using the talks as a platform for revolutionary propaganda (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Creation of the Red Army**: Outlines his work in organizing the Red Army from scratch during the Civil War to defend the Soviet state against the White armies and foreign interventionists (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **The Tsaritsyn Opposition**: Documents the bitter conflict with Joseph Stalin and Kliment Voroshilov over military centralization and the use of former Tsarist specialists during the [[battle-of-tsaritsyn|Battle of Tsaritsyn]], which Trotsky describes as the genesis of the Stalinist faction and Stalin's subsequent rise to power (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **German Revolution and the International Ebb-Tide**: Recalls the strategic primacy of the German revolution (especially during the Brest-Litovsk talks) and how the 1923 defeat in Germany led to international disillusionment, fostering the rise of the Stalinist bureaucracy and the policy of "socialism in one country" at the expense of the Left Opposition and "permanent revolution" (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **The Georgian Affair**: Recalls his collaboration with Lenin in early 1923 to oppose Stalin's bureaucratic centralization and heavy-handed intervention in Georgia, culminating in Lenin's aborted "bomb" for the Twelfth Party Congress and break in personal relations with Stalin (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **The New Economic Policy (NEP)**: Outlines his early February 1920 proposal to replace requisitioning with a tax in kind to restore market relations, its rejection by Lenin's Central Committee, and the subsequent trade union dispute that arose before the NEP's final adoption in 1921 (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **The Trade Union Debate**: Recalls the intense late 1920 factional struggle concerning the role of trade unions in the Soviet state, where Trotsky advocated for integrating unions into industrial administration, while Lenin saw them as a "school of communism" (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Factional Struggles and Exile**: Documents his close collaboration with Lenin and his subsequent political defeat by the rising Soviet bureaucracy led by [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]], which co-opted state power, fabricated history, and expelled him from the USSR (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
