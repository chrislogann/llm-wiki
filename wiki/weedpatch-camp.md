# Weedpatch Camp

**Summary**: A federally funded, self-governing migrant camp in California that provided a model of dignity and community for displaced workers.

**Sources**: [The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md](file:///home/chrislogann/Desktop/repository/llm-wiki/raw/The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-09

---

Weedpatch Camp (officially the Arvin Migratory Labor Camp) was established by the Farm Security Administration (FSA) in the Central Valley of California (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md). The camp was designed to provide sanitary housing, clean water, and medical care to migrant families fleeing the [[dust-bowl]] (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).

## Self-Governance Model
Unlike private grower camps, Weedpatch was run democratically by the migrants themselves through committees (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md). The residents established their own rules, resolved disputes, and managed camp maintenance and social events (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md). This self-government restored self-respect and disproved the growers' claims that the migrants were lawless and animalistic (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).

## Literary Significance
In [[the-grapes-of-wrath]], the Joad family stays at Weedpatch Camp, which serves as a temporary haven and a symbol of what cooperative, democratic society could look like (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md). The character of the camp manager, Jim Rawley, was based on Tom Collins, the real-life FSA manager who assisted [[john-steinbeck|Steinbeck]] during his research (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).
