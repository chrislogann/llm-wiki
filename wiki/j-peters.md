# [[j-peters|J. Peters]]

**Summary**: The head of the CPUSA secret underground apparatus in the 1930s and author of the *Peters Manual*, who directed the [[ware-group|Ware Group]] and coordinated Soviet GRU intelligence networks in Washington D.C.

**Sources**: `Witness_9780895267894.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

[[j-peters|J. Peters]] (1894–1990), born Sándor Goldberger (and also known by the alias Alexander Stevens), was a Hungarian-born Communist organizer who served as the chief administrator of the CPUSA’s secret underground apparatus (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). In 1935, he published the *Peters Manual on Party Organization*, which served as the official guide for CPUSA organizational structure (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Director of the Washington Underground
During the 1930s, Peters managed the party's secret cells in Washington D.C. (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). He oversaw the [[ware-group|Ware Group]] after the death of Harold Ware in 1935, using it to infiltrate New Deal agencies (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

Peters worked closely with GRU courier [[whittaker-chambers|Whittaker Chambers]] to siphon off high-level government employees from the political cells into active military espionage, including [[alger-hiss|Alger Hiss]] (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Under Peters' direction, the underground network expanded to the point where he boasted that even under the Weimar Republic, the German Communist Party did not possess such a highly placed network (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Exposure and Deportation
Peters' role as the head of the CPUSA underground was exposed by [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] during his HUAC testimony in August 1948 (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Subpoenaed to testify, Peters refused to answer questions, citing his Fifth Amendment rights (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

Because of his underground activities and illegal alien status, the US government initiated deportation proceedings against him (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). In 1949, Peters voluntarily left the United States for Communist-run Hungary, where he spent the rest of his life under the name József Péter, managing state publications until his death in Budapest in 1990 (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).
