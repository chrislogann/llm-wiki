# Decomposition

**Summary**: Decomposition is the process of breaking down a complex problem or system into smaller, more manageable parts that are easier to understand, program, and maintain.

**Sources**: `What is decomposition - Decomposition - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Decomposition is a cornerstone of [[computational-thinking]], enabling developers to tackle large, complex problems by partitioning them into simpler, independent components (source: What is decomposition - Decomposition - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).

## Practice and Importance

- **Manageability**: Attempting to solve a large problem all at once is extremely difficult. Breaking it down allows each small part to be examined and solved individually (source: What is decomposition - Decomposition - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).
- **Understanding Complex Systems**: Decomposition is also useful for analyzing existing systems. For example, understanding how a bicycle works becomes much more straightforward when it is separated into smaller components (like the brakes, chain, and pedals) to be examined in detail (source: What is decomposition - Decomposition - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).
