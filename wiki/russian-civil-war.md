# Russian Civil War

**Summary**: The Russian Civil War (1917-1921) was a multi-party war in the former Russian Empire immediately after the Russian Revolutions of 1917, resulting in the victory of the Bolsheviks and the creation of the Soviet Union.

**Sources**: `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`, `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The Russian Civil War was fought between the Bolshevik Red Army and the loose alliance of anti-Bolshevik forces known as the White Army, alongside various regional national movements, foreign interventionists, and independent peasant armies (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). In imperial terms, it represented the military reconquest of the former Tsarist Empire by Vladimir Lenin and [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]], laying the territorial foundation for the Soviet state (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Factions and Dynamics

- **The Reds**: The Bolshevik government held the central industrial heartland of Russia (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Under the military leadership of [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]], the Red Army was built from scratch and directed through a highly centralized command system, often coordinated by Trotsky from his specialized armored train (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **The Whites**: Led by monarchist and nationalist officers (such as Kolchak, Denikin, and Wrangel), they occupied the periphery but suffered from deep internal divisions, lack of a unified political vision, and logistical challenges (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
- **Imperial Reconquest and Comintern Founding**: As the Red Army re-established control over Ukraine, the Caucasus (Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan), Central Asia, and Siberia, Lenin simultaneously established the [[comintern|Comintern]] in March 1919 to coordinate foreign communist parties and export the revolution globally (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## The Atamanshchina (Reign of the Atamans)

In Siberia, beyond Lake Baikal, the civil war deteriorated into the *atamanshchina*—the rule of terror by regional Cossack warlords:
- **Grigory Semenov**: Established a base in Chita on the Trans-Siberian Railway, running a regime of corruption, looting, and regional terror (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
- **[[roman-von-ungern-sternberg|Roman von Ungern-Sternberg]]**: Known as the "Mad Baron," he ruled with extreme cruelty, executing Bolsheviks, Jews, and any who opposed him, before launching his 1920-1921 invasion of Mongolia, which provoked the Red Army intervention that created the first Soviet satellite in Outer Mongolia (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md, source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
- **Peasant Alienation**: The horrific excesses and anti-partisan sweeps of these atamans (such as hanging hostages on telegraph poles along the Trans-Siberian Railway) alienated the conservative Siberian peasantry, driving them to support the Bolsheviks and guaranteeing a Red victory (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## Border Resentment and Red Overtures

White warlords along the southern flank provoked deep resentment among Chinese border authorities due to their imperialistic and anarchic militarism, which recalled the humiliations of the late Qing Empire (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). The Reds exploited this by acting far more intelligently, using anti-imperialist rhetoric to build positive diplomatic relationships with China (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
