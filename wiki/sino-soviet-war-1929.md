# 1929 Sino-Soviet War - The War Nobody Knew

**Summary**: A summary of Michael Walker's history of the 1929 Sino-Soviet War, detailing the conflict over the Chinese Eastern Railroad and the strategic use of propaganda, radio, and military force.

**Sources**: `1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Michael Walker's *1929 Sino-Soviet War* provides a detailed study of the brief but significant border conflict between the Republic of China (under Chiang Kai-shek and Chang Hsueh-liang) and the Soviet Union under Joseph Stalin (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

## Key Themes

- **The Conflict over the Chinese Eastern Railroad (CER)**: The war was triggered in July 1929 when Chinese authorities seized control of the jointly managed [[chinese-eastern-railroad|Chinese Eastern Railroad]] to recover national sovereignty and halt Soviet communist subversion (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **The Soviet Red Army (ODVA) Offensive**: Commanded by Vasily Blyukher, the Special Far Eastern Army (ODVA) launched a highly coordinated offensive, crushing the Chinese defenses on both the Eastern and Western fronts, forcing a total Chinese capitulation (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **The Propaganda and Psychological War**: Both sides waged intensive propaganda campaigns:
  - The Soviet Union fabricated a "White Russian terrorist" threat (the "White boogeyman") to justify military incursions under the Kellogg-Briand Pact (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
  - The Soviets deployed Khabarovsk radio broadcasts targeting Harbin, representing an early historical use of radio as an offensive propaganda weapon (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
  - The Chinese Communist Party (CCP) launched unpopular pro-Soviet campaigns inside China to prove its absolute loyalty to Stalin, securing vital Soviet support for the Chinese revolution (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **The Legacy of the "Mad Baron"**: The book traces the Soviet presence in Outer Mongolia back to the 1921 campaigns against [[roman-von-ungern-sternberg|Roman von Ungern-Sternberg]] (the "Mad Baron"), which led to the creation of the Mongolian People's Republic (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
