# Roman von Ungern-Sternberg

**Summary**: Baron Roman F. von Ungern-Sternberg (known as the "Mad Baron") was a Baltic-German tsarist officer who became a key figure of the *atamanshchina* terror in Siberia and captured Urga (Mongolia) in 1921. His subsequent defeat by Soviet forces led directly to the creation of the Mongolian People's Republic.

**Sources**: `1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md`, `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Baron Roman F. von Ungern-Sternberg was an "intelligent psychopath" from a family of Baltic barons (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). He was a veteran of the Russo-Japanese War and a Cossack cavalry officer who practiced Lamaist Buddhism (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

## Psychopathology and Command Habits

Ungern-Sternberg was notorious for his eccentric and brutal behavior:
- **Hatred of Bureaucracy**: He despised regulations and paperwork, throwing all official correspondence straight into his stove (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
- **Ascetic Lifestyle**: He avoided the company of women and never spent money on himself, instead keeping wolves in his house in Dauria (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
- **Dauria Execution**: On New Year's Eve 1917, he arrested and executed a drunken Bolshevik commissar (sailor Kudryashev) at Dauria, seizing 200,000 roubles to pay his men (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## The Atamanshchina (Reign of the Atamans)

During the [[russian-civil-war|Russian Civil War]], Ungern-Sternberg broke away from Grigory Semenov in the Trans-Baikal region (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). He and Semenov co-founded a cavalry brigade (which eventually grew to 4,500 sabres) comprising Cossacks, Buryats, and Mongols to establish a regional state (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). 

Ungern-Sternberg became a central figure in the *atamanshchina* (the rule of terror of the Cossack atamans), characterized by extreme cruelty, torture, and obsessive anti-Semitism (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Along with other warlords, his imperialistic and anarchic behavior provoked deep resentment among the Chinese authorities (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## Invasion of Mongolia and Capture of Urga

In late 1920, leading the Asiatic Cavalry Division of about 1,000 White soldiers, he invaded Outer Mongolia to escape the Red advance (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

- **The Fall of Urga**: On February 3, 1921, Ungern-Sternberg captured the Mongolian capital of Urga (Ulaanbaatar), freeing the Bogd Khan (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **Atrocities**: Following his victory, he ordered the massacre of the Chinese garrison, local Jewish populations, and anyone deemed undesirable by his regime (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

## Defeat and Execution

In mid-1921, the Soviet Red Army (PRA) under Vasily Blyukher, alongside the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Army (MPRA) under Sukhbaatar, launched a counter-offensive (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

- **Lake Gusinoe**: Ungern-Sternberg's forces were decisively defeated at Kyakhta and later at Lake Gusinoe (by the 104th Rifle Regiment) in July 1921 (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
- **Mutiny and Death**: Facing defeat, he proposed to march his remaining 2,000 men to Tibet, causing a mutiny in which his loyalists were killed (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). He was seized on August 20, 1921, turned over to the Soviets, and executed on September 15, 1921 (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).

## Geopolitical Consequences

Blyukher's campaign against the Mad Baron resulted in the permanent loss of Chinese control over Outer Mongolia, culminating in the establishment of the pro-Soviet Mongolian People's Republic on November 26, 1924 (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md).
