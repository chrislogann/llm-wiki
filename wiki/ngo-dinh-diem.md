# [[ngo-dinh-diem|Ngo Dinh Diem]]

**Summary**: The first President of South Vietnam, who established an autocratic, nepotistic, and strongly anti-communist regime with US backing, before being assassinated during a military coup in 1963.

**Sources**: `Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

[[ngo-dinh-diem|Ngo Dinh Diem]] (1901–1963) was the President of the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam) from 1955 until his assassination in 1963 (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). Born into a prominent Catholic mandarin family in central Vietnam, Diem was a staunch nationalist and anti-communist (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Rise to Power
Diem served as Interior Minister under the French-backed Emperor Bao Dai in 1933 but resigned in protest against French refusal to grant real autonomy (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). In 1954, following the **[[geneva-accords-1954|Geneva Accords]]**, Bao Dai appointed Diem as Prime Minister (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

With the support of the United States, Diem consolidated power by crushing the armed religious sects (the Binh Xuyen, Cao Dai, and Hoa Hao) and ousting Bao Dai in a rigged 1955 referendum, declaring himself President of the newly formed Republic of Vietnam (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Autocratic Rule and Nepotism
Diem established a highly centralized, nepotistic regime (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). His closest adviser was his brother, Ngo Dinh Nhu, who controlled the secret police (the Can Lao party), while his sister-in-law, Madame Nhu, acted as the controversial first lady (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

Diem refused to hold the national reunification elections scheduled for 1956 by the [[geneva-accords-1954|Geneva Accords]], fearing a victory by [[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]] (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). His regime launched the "Denounce Communists" campaign, executing and imprisoning tens of thousands of suspected political opponents, which drove moderate nationalists into the arms of the Viet Cong insurgency (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## The Buddhist Crisis and Assassination
As a devout Catholic, Diem heavily favored the Catholic minority in government appointments, land distribution, and military promotions, alienating the country's Buddhist majority (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). In May 1963, government troops fired on Buddhist demonstrators in Hue who were protesting a ban on flying religious flags (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

The crisis escalated when Buddhist monks (most famously Thich Quang Duc) publicly self-immolated in protest, and Nhu's special forces raided Buddhist pagodas across South Vietnam (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). Convinced that Diem could no longer defeat the communist insurgency, the administration of US President John F. Kennedy signaled support for a military coup (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). On November 1, 1963, South Vietnamese generals overthrew the regime; Diem and Nhu were captured and assassinated the following day (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).
