# Wyandotte Constitution

**Summary**: The 1859 state constitution drafted in Wyandotte that established Kansas as a free state and served as the foundation for its admission to the Union in 1861.

**Sources**: [The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

The Wyandotte Constitution was the fourth and final constitution drafted during the "Bleeding Kansas" era, succeeding the Topeka, Lecompton, and Leavenworth constitutions (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). 

## Wyandotte Convention (July 1859)
The convention convened on July 5, 1859, in Wyandotte City (present-day Kansas City, Kansas) under the control of the Free-State party (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). The delegation was characterized by intense factional debates regarding apportionment, state boundaries, and voting rights:
- **Boundaries**: The convention established the state's northern boundary at the 40th parallel (rejecting attempts to incorporate the Platte River country of southern Nebraska) and its western boundary at the 102nd meridian (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
- **Prohibition of Slavery**: The constitution explicitly prohibited slavery within the state, ensuring that Kansas would enter the Union as a free state (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Ratification and Statehood
The constitution was submitted to the electorate on October 4, 1859, and was ratified by a large majority (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). This ratification cleared the way for the election of state officers in December 1859, including Governor [[charles-robinson|Charles Robinson]] (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). After delays in Congress, the Wyandotte Constitution became the supreme law of the state when Kansas was officially admitted to the Union on January 29, 1861 (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
