# Comparison: The Life of a Salesman and Nazi Propaganda

**Summary**: A comparative analysis of the life of a salesman under monopoly capitalism (as represented by George F. Babbitt and 1920s American advertising) and the mechanics of Nazi propaganda (as detailed in Willi Münzenberg's *Propaganda als Waffe*).

While one operates in the sphere of liberal-democratic consumerism and the other under a totalitarian dictatorship, the **life of the American salesman** in the 1920s and the execution of **Nazi propaganda** share deep structural, psychological, and commercial affinities. Both represent methods of mass manipulation designed to sustain centralized power—monopoly capital in the US, and the fascist dictatorship in Germany.

## 1. The Salesman as a Packaged Commodity

In the 1920s American business system, the salesman was not just a distributor of goods, but the **"evangel" of capitalist enterprise**. 
* **The Death of Individuality**: As exemplified by Sinclair Lewis's character George F. Babbitt, the salesman's life, preferences, and identity were completely fixed by national advertisers. Standardized products (toothpastes, socks, alarm clocks) became "substitutes for joy, passion, and wisdom."
* **Selling the Self**: To "break down consumer resistance," the salesman was forced to commodify and package his own personality. Honesty, ethics, and friendly boosterism became tools of trade, calculated strictly to build confidence and "handle bigger propositions."
* **Mass Passivity**: The salesman's existence depended on a public that acted as passive consumers rather than active citizens. The individual was reduced to a unit of consumption, whose desires were constantly stoked to prevent industrial overproduction.

## 2. Nazi Propaganda as Commercial Advertising (*Reklame*)

Willi Münzenberg's *Propaganda als Waffe* details how Hitler and the NSDAP explicitly treated propaganda as a form of **mass commercial advertising** rather than intellectual education.
* **The Masses as Raw Material**: Goebbels viewed the public as an unformed "mass" or "raw material" to be shaped by the "state artist." Like the consumer targeted by advertisers, the citizen was treated as an uncritical vessel governed by emotion and primitive instincts rather than intellect.
* **The Political Commodity (The Hitler Myth)**: Just as advertisers sold Sani-White toilet seats or Cream of Wheat by promising democratic equality, Goebbels sold the person of Adolf Hitler. The Fūhrer was packaged as a religious Messianic figure to bypass critical evaluation and secure blind devotion.
* **Plagiarism of Commercial Techniques**: Nazi planners openly admitted that they learned their methods from Allied WWI propaganda and corporate advertising, adopting slogans, simplifying complex issues, and repeating falsehoods until they were believed.

## 3. Comparative Synthesis: The Managed Mind

| Theme | The Life of a Salesman (American Monopoly Capital) | Nazi Propaganda (Totalitarian Fascism) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **View of the Human Being** | A consumer to be cajoled, exhorted, or intimidated into buying. | A mass cell of the social organism to be directed, commanded, and controlled. |
| **Role of Ethics/Truth** | Instrumentalized as "ethics" and "service" to win consumer confidence and make sales. | Rejected in favor of absolute opportunism; success is the only metric of truth. |
| **Nature of the Commodity** | Mass-produced physical goods that promise personal status, comfort, and equality. | The political myth of the Fūhrer, national destiny, and militarized unity. |
| **Manipulative Illusion** | The *Democracy of Goods*: equal access to commodities makes actual class divisions irrelevant. | The *Volksgemeinschaft*: belonging to the national collective replaces material redistribution. |

## Conclusion

Both the American salesman and the Nazi propagandist operate on the fundamental assumption that human consciousness can be scientifically managed and engineered. Under monopoly capitalism, the salesman commodifies himself to sell goods, integrating business into civic religion. Under fascism, the propagandist uses the very same commercial techniques to commodify the state, turning citizens into soldiers and politics into a totalizing, theatrical commodity.
