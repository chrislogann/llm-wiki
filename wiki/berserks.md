# Berserks

**Summary**: Ancient Norse warriors known for entering a god-fueled frenzy, whose imagery was co-opted in Nazi propaganda to frame the Norwegian collaborationist war effort as righteous and divinely protected.

**Sources**: `Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

In Norse history and mythology, berserks (or berserkers) were elite warriors who entered battle in a state of wild, god-fueled frenzy (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). They were reputed to fight without feeling the bite of steel or fire, appearing to be protected by the blessings of the gods rather than physical armor (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).

## Use in Nazi Propaganda

Nazi recruitment posters in occupied Norway utilized this motif by depicting a shirtless, god-like warrior fighting unarmed or unprotected against "our country's enemies" (sabotage, terror, murder, and the red front) (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). 
- By linking recruitment to the mythical berserk archetype, the propaganda aimed to frame collaboration with the Nazis as a righteous, divinely sanctioned duty that transcended the need for conventional armor (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
- This representation further contributed to the post-war stigma and general public ignorance regarding the actual religious and cultural context of Norse berserks (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
