# Abstraction

**Summary**: Abstraction is the process of filtering out or ignoring irrelevant details and characteristics, allowing developers to focus solely on the information necessary to solve a problem and build a general model.

**Sources**: `What is abstraction - Abstraction - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Abstraction allows computer scientists to manage complexity by removing specific details that do not affect the general solution of the problem at hand (source: What is abstraction - Abstraction - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).

## Filtering Specific Details

- **Creating a Model**: By removing unnecessary details and focusing only on core characteristics, developers can create a simplified representation of the problem, known as a **model** (source: What is abstraction - Abstraction - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).
- **The Cat Example**: To represent or draw a basic cat, one needs to know that a cat has eyes, a tail, and fur (relevant details). However, details about the cat's favorite food, the sounds it makes, or the specific color of its eyes are irrelevant and are filtered out during abstraction (source: What is abstraction - Abstraction - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).
- **Avoiding Wrong Solutions**: Without abstraction, developers risk building overly specific models (e.g., assuming all cats must have short fur and long tails), which leads to incorrect or fragile solutions (source: What is abstraction - Abstraction - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).
