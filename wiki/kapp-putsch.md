# Kapp Putsch

**Summary**: The Kapp Putsch of March 1920 was a failed right-wing military coup against the Weimar Republic that was defeated by a massive general strike and armed working-class resistance.

**Sources**: `Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

The Kapp Putsch began on March 13, 1920, when the heavily armed Ehrhardt Brigade and other right-wing forces marched into Berlin, forcing the Social Democrat-led government to flee (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). The coup leaders, including General Walther von Lüttwitz and bureaucrat Wolfgang Kapp, proclaimed a new conservative government (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).

## Seeckt's Refusal and the Fleeing Government

Noske, the minister of war, had spent the previous year reinstating the old monarchist officer corps to crush left-wing uprisings (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). When he ordered his generals to stop the coup, they refused. Hans von Seeckt, the head of the army, famously stated: *"Reichswehr will not fire on Reichswehr"* (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). Noske and President Friedrich Ebert were forced to flee to Dresden and then Stuttgart (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).

## The General Strike and Red Ruhr Army

Despite its military success, the coup was defeated within days by the working class:
- **General Strike**: Trade union leader Carl Legien called for a national general strike, paralyzing Berlin and the rest of Germany within 24 hours (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).
- **Armed Resistance**: In the Ruhr valley and central Germany, workers formed the "Red Ruhr Army," disarming local troops and police, and establishing temporary workers' councils (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).
- **Collapse**: Trapped by the silent power of the strike and the armed counter-offensive, Kapp and Lüttwitz resigned and fled on March 17 (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).

## Aftermath and Legacy

Following the collapse of the putsch, the Social Democrat leaders returned to office and reconstructed the government. Rather than purging the monarchist military, they reunited with the right-wing generals to crush the armed workers' councils that had saved them (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). Within months, the right-wing ousted the SPD from government (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).

The Ehrhardt Brigade and other Freikorps units involved in the putsch were characterized by their use of the swastika as an emblem painted on their helmets, representing the symbol's first major entry into modern German political violence (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).
