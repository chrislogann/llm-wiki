# Chechnya: A Small Victorious War

**Summary**: A detailed history and analysis of the [[first-chechen-war|First Chechen War]] (1994–1996) by Carlotta Gall and Thomas de Waal, documenting its deep historical origins, political triggers in Moscow, the siege of Grozny, and the final Russian military defeat.

**Sources**: `Chechnya_9780330350754.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

*Chechnya: A Small Victorious War* (published in 1997) is an account of the conflict between Russia and the breakaway republic of Chechnya during the mid-1990s. The authors, Carlotta Gall and Thomas de Waal, reported extensively from the ground, providing both political analysis of the decision-making in Yeltsin's Kremlin and harrowing eyewitness accounts of the fighting and its impact on the civilian population (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

## Key Themes

### 1. Historical Grievance and Operations
The book traces the conflict back to historical resistance under [[jokhar-dudayev|Jokhar Dudayev]], who drew inspiration from Imam Shamil and the 19th-century Caucasian Wars (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). A critical event fueling modern Chechen nationalism was the Soviet mass [[chechen-deportations|deportations of 1944]] under [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]], during which the entire Chechen population was exiled to Central Asia (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### 2. [[jokhar-dudayev|Dudayev]]'s Breakaway Regime
Following the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991, retired Soviet Air Force General [[jokhar-dudayev|Jokhar Dudayev]] seized power and declared Chechen independence. The regime survived for three years despite Russian attempts at economic blockade, engaging in lucrative oil trading and weapon smuggling, often with the complicity of corrupt Russian officials (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### 3. The "Small Victorious War" and the Invasion
In late 1994, facing domestic unpopularity, Russian President Boris Yeltsin and a small circle of hawkish advisers (including Defence Minister Pavel Grachev) launched the invasion of Chechnya. Grachev infamously boasted that a single paratroop regiment could capture Grozny in two hours. Instead, the invasion on New Year's Eve 1994 resulted in a tactical disaster, beginning the [[first-chechen-war|First Chechen War]] (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### 4. Siege and Asymmetric Warfare
The Russian army resorted to indiscriminate artillery bombardment and airstrikes, reducing Grozny to rubble and killing thousands of civilians (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). Chechen fighters, led by commanders like Chief of Staff [[aslan-maskhadov|Aslan Maskhadov]], responded with highly effective guerrilla tactics. To force negotiations, field commander [[shamil-basayev|Shamil Basayev]] launched a massive hostage-taking raid in Budyonnovsk in 1995, exposing Russian military impotence and shifting public opinion (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### 5. Russian Defeat and Withdrawal
Despite killing [[jokhar-dudayev|Dudayev]] with a guided missile in April 1996, the Russian forces could not secure the territory. In August 1996, Chechen fighters under [[aslan-maskhadov|Aslan Maskhadov]] launched a surprise offensive, recapturing Grozny. This forced Russian General Alexander Lebed to sign the Khasavyurt Accord, leading to the complete withdrawal of Russian forces (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

## Chapter Structure
1. **New Year’s Eve**: The disastrous Russian armored assault on Grozny on December 31, 1994.
2. **The French of the Caucasus**: An introduction to Chechen culture, society, and their reputation for independence.
3. **Conquest and Resistance**: Historical background of Russian conquest in the Caucasus and the resistance led by Imam Shamil.
4. **The Deportations**: The trauma of Stalin's 1944 deportation of the Chechen people.
5. **[[jokhar-dudayev|Dudayev]]’s Revolution**: The political rise of Jokhar [[jokhar-dudayev|Dudayev]] in 1991.
6. **Independent Chechnya**: The chaotic, unrecognized sovereign state from 1991 to 1994.
7. **A Free Economic Zone**: The shadow economy, oil trade, and financial fraud that funded the regime.
8. **A Small Victorious War**: The Kremlin decision-making process leading to the December 1994 invasion.
9. **Russia Invades**: The initial military deployment and early resistance.
10. **The Battle for Grozny**: The destruction and capture of the capital city.
11. **War Against the People**: Human rights abuses, filtration camps, and civilian suffering.
12. **Terrorism and Talks**: Shamil [[shamil-basayev|Basayev]]'s raid on Budyonnovsk and subsequent peace negotiations.
13. **‘A Semi-Guerrilla War’**: The spread of the conflict into the mountains.
14. **Death in the Gully**: The assassination of Jokhar [[jokhar-dudayev|Dudayev]].
15. **Grozny Recaptured**: The August 1996 counter-offensive and the Khasavyurt Accord.
