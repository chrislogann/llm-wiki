# Golden City

**Summary**: The historical nickname for Topeka, Kansas, originating from an 1850s Pottawatomie vision of the setting sun reflecting off the city's glass windows.

**Sources**: [Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

In local history, Topeka, Kansas is referred to as the **"Golden City"** (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). The name stems from a specific visual and spiritual event recorded in the late 1850s (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## The Pottawatomie Vision
According to Mary E. Jackson's compilation of local legends, a band of Pottawatomie Indians was returning from a hunting expedition near the end of the 1850s (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). While resting on their ponies on a high ridge west of Topeka (near the site of the state asylum), they looked down at the white man's settlement in the valley as the autumn sun was setting (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). 

Because the sun was low and casting deep golden and red rays, the sunlight reflected off the glass windows of the newly built houses and buildings, causing the entire city to appear as one blaze of dazzling brightness (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). To the observers, the physical city seemed to disappear, replaced by a sparkling, multicolored gem of pale blue, crimson, rose, and amber light (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## Spiritual Significance
Believing the spectacle was a sign that the Great Spirit had manifested to bless the inhabitants of the city, the Pottawatomie folded their arms and asked the Great Spirit to bless the white man in his "golden city" (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Jackson adopted the nickname to describe the city's early promise and growth (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).
