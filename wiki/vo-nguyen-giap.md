# [[vo-nguyen-giap|Vo Nguyen Giap]]

**Summary**: The principal military commander of the Viet Minh and the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN), who orchestrated the decisive victory at Dien Bien Phu and directed the military strategy that defeated the United States.

**Sources**: `Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

[[vo-nguyen-giap|Vo Nguyen Giap]] (1911–2013) was a self-taught Vietnamese military strategist and the commander-in-chief of North Vietnam's armed forces (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). A former history teacher and journalist with a law degree, Giap joined [[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]]'s movement in China in 1940 after his wife died in a French prison (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Architect of the Viet Minh Military
In 1944, Giap was tasked by Ho to organize the first armed propaganda units of the Viet Minh in the mountains of northern Vietnam (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). During the **[[first-indochina-war|First Indochina War]]**, Giap developed a three-phase military doctrine adapted from Mao Zedong’s theories of revolutionary warfare (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md):
1. **Defensive retreat** and consolidation.
2. **Guerrilla warfare** to harass and bleed enemy forces.
3. **General offensive** using conventional forces when superiority is achieved.

## The Victory at Dien Bien Phu
Giap’s masterpiece was the **[[battle-of-dien-bien-phu|Battle of Dien Bien Phu]]** in 1954 (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). Challenging French assumptions that heavy artillery could not be deployed in the mountainous jungle, Giap mobilized tens of thousands of peasants to drag dismantled heavy guns up sheer cliffs on foot (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). By surrounding and bombarding the French valley garrison, he forced their surrender, ending the French empire in Indochina (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Strategy Against the United States
During the Vietnam War, Giap served as Defense Minister and commander of the PAVN (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). He organized the construction of the [[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]] Trail to supply southern insurgents and coordinated the logistics of the conflict (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). Giap was willing to accept staggering casualties, calculating that the political will of the United States would collapse before that of North Vietnam (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). His strategies, including the 1968 Tet Offensive, ultimately forced US withdrawal (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).
