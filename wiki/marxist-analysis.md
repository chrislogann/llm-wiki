# Marxist Analysis

**Summary**: An analytical framework that views political and historical developments primarily through the lens of class struggle and economic structures.

**Sources**: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md, The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Marxist analysis often approaches historical phenomena, such as [[national-socialism]], by focusing on structural economic drivers rather than cultural or ideological ones.

In [[behemoth]], Franz Neumann applies a Marxist perspective, interpreting the Third Reich as an "imperialist" movement serving large-scale capitalism's needs for markets and resources (source: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md). This perspective tends to see policies as mechanisms to consolidate class power and suppress the proletariat, sometimes missing the deeply held, albeit irrational, ideological commitments of the actors involved.

In the context of antebellum America, Marxist analysis interprets the chattel slavery system depicted in [[the-annotated-uncle-toms-cabin|Uncle Tom's Cabin]] not as a pre-capitalist anomaly, but as a core mode of production integrated into global capitalist industrialism, providing the cheap cotton necessary for Northern and British textile manufacturing (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). From this perspective, the legal, political, and religious justifications for slavery constituted an ideological superstructure built to protect the economic interests and property relations of the slave-owning class (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).
