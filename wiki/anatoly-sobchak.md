# Anatoly Sobchak

**Summary**: A prominent Soviet and Russian reformist politician, legal scholar, and the first Mayor of St. Petersburg who mentored Vladimir Putin.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-16

---

Anatoly Sobchak (1937–2000) was a prominent Soviet and Russian reformist politician, legal scholar, and the first democratically elected Mayor of St. Petersburg. He played a pivotal role in the transition of Leningrad into post-Soviet St. Petersburg and was the political mentor of [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]].

## Early Career and Rise to Prominence
Before entering politics, Sobchak was a professor of law at Leningrad State University (LSU). He gained national prominence in March 1989 when he was elected to the newly created Congress of People's Deputies of the USSR. As a deputy, he joined the reformist Inter-Regional Deputies Group alongside figures like Andrei Sakharov and [[boris-yeltsin|Boris Yeltsin]].

Sobchak chaired the congressional commission appointed by the Congress of People's Deputies of the USSR that investigated the April 9, 1989, Tbilisi massacre (or "gas canister massacre") in Georgia, where Soviet military forces killed 20 peaceful demonstrators using chemical weapons and violent tactics (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Sobchak, who was noted to be a friend of [[boris-yeltsin|Boris Yeltsin]], led the commission to establish that the Georgian Party boss Dzhumber Patiashvili had bypassed Mikhail Gorbachev and Eduard Shevardnadze to appeal to hardliner Egor Ligachev for interior and regular military troops. The operation was subsequently executed under the command of General Igor Rodionov with the backing of KGB chief Viktor Chebrikov and Defense Minister Dmitri Yazov. Ultimately, Sobchak's commission exonerated both Gorbachev and [[boris-yeltsin|Yeltsin]] from all blame (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Mayor of St. Petersburg (1990–1996)
In May 1990, Sobchak became Chairman of the Leningrad City Council. Lacking administrative experience and suspicious of the existing bureaucracy, he sought a trustworthy assistant to act as a liaison to the security apparatus. He recruited LSU alumnus and active KGB officer [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] as an advisor (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

In June 1991, Sobchak was elected Mayor of Leningrad. In the same election, voters approved a referendum to rename the city St. Petersburg. During the [[august-coup-1991|August 1991 coup attempt]] by hardline Soviet officials, Sobchak organized the local resistance, successfully persuading the military commander General Viktor Samsonov not to deploy troops in the city and rallying 130,000 citizens in Palace Square to support democracy (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Administrative Controversies and Putin Partnership
Sobchak's mayoral administration was marked by growing friction with the city council, which accused him of authoritarian methods. He frequently bypassed reformists to hire Soviet apparatchiks and security veterans.

Key controversies during his administration included:
- **Gambling Industry Regulation:** Under Sobchak's decree, Putin was put in charge of regulating the city's rapidly expanding casino industry. This led to the creation of the municipal enterprise *Neva Chance* (co-advised by LSU lawyer Dmitri Medvedev), which failed to bring in city revenues and became associated with money laundering and organized crime (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
- **Economic Transition:** St. Petersburg faced severe food shortages in the winter of 1991. The administration's attempts to establish a free economic zone met with limited success and faced accusations of corruption.
- **The 1993 Constitutional Crisis:** During the [[russian-constitutional-crisis-1993|1993 Russian Constitutional Crisis]], Sobchak sided decisively with Yeltsin, locking his vice mayor Vyacheslav Shcherbakov out of office for supporting the parliament. He sent St. Petersburg special forces to Moscow to aid Yeltsin, and consolidated mayoral authority over the city council following the crisis (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Downfall, Exile, and Death
Sobchak lost his mayoral re-election campaign in 1996 to his deputy Vladimir Yakovlev. Following his defeat, he was targeted by corruption investigations regarding the privatization of St. Petersburg apartments and properties, spearheaded by Prosecutor General Yuri Skuratov. In November 1997, amid threats of arrest, Sobchak fled to France with the assistance of Putin (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

Sobchak returned to Russia in 1999 after Putin became Prime Minister and the criminal cases against him were dropped. He died of a heart attack in Kaliningrad on February 18, 2000, while campaigning for Putin's presidential race (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Putin delivered the eulogy at his funeral, calling him "our master."

Sobchak's widow, Lyudmila Narusova, later recalled his final warning to Putin during the 2000 campaign: "Volodya, don't become bronzed," warning him against the corrupting influence of unchecked power (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
