# A "Common Madness"

**Summary**: An article examining the rise of TV psychics in the late Soviet Union, linking their massive popularity to parapsychological research, sports suggestion training, media liberalization under Glasnost, and the collapse of the state healthcare system.

**Sources**: `A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-18

---

## Media Liberalization vs. State Control
Scholar Wladimir Velminski has argued that televised séances by psychics like [[anatoly-kashpirovsky]] were a coordinated Party effort to psychologically manipulate and calm a public panicked by political upheaval (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md). However, the author argues a more convincing explanation lies in Perestroika-era media liberalization (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md):
* **Ratings-Driven Television**: As the Communist Party's grip on media loosened, Gosteleradio transitioned from seeking ideological alignment to actively chasing audience ratings, broadcasting psychics alongside Western pop concerts and soap operas (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Mass Audience Receptivity**: A 1990 VTsIOM poll showed that 57% of viewers put aside all activities to watch Kashpirovsky's séances, empty streets matching the popularity of Soviet blockbusters (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).

## Scientific Roots: Parapsychology and Suggestion
Rather than a sudden collapse of rationalism, the psychics emerged from long-running debates and military-backed research within the Soviet scientific establishment (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md):
* **Early Telepathy**: In the 1920s, neurologist Vladimir Bekhterev investigated telepathic communication (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Biofields (1970s)**: Soviet laboratories, funded by military intelligence, used military and astronomical sensors to detect biological energy fields ("biofields") to explain extra-sensory perception (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Emotional-Volitional Sports Training**: Soviet sports psychology tested suggestion to optimize athletic performance. Kashpirovsky worked closely as a hypnotherapist with the Soviet weightlifting team that won ten golds at the 1987 World Championships, teaching them to trust their body's innate self-healing capabilities—a style he transferred directly to TV (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).

## Healthcare Collapse and Spiritual Confusion
* **Healthcare Shortages**: Turn toward psychics was a logical, pragmatic choice for families. While medical care was ostensibly free, corruption and shortages of basic medicines meant pharmacies were empty, and patients were forced to wait hours for treatment unless they paid bribes (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Spiritual Hollowing**: The fatal discredit of communist ideology created a "spiritual confusion." The public became highly suggestible, embracing astrology, white magic, and Scientology (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Lenin was a Mushroom**: This suggestibility was famously demonstrated in 1991 when musician Sergei Kurekhin broadcast a famous television prank asserting that Vladimir Lenin's genetic makeup had transformed into a mushroom due to consuming psychoactive mushrooms, prompting panicked viewers to call Leningrad broadcasters to verify the story (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).

## Health Risks and Cancellation
In early 1990, Kashpirovsky's television program was canceled following growing concerns (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md):
* **Medical Complications**: *Pravda* reported a sharp spike in cardiovascular complaints, hypertension, and psychological crises following séances. The Ministry of Health cited the case of a 30-year-old diabetic patient who suffered psychosis and hallucinations after watching, ending up in an artificial coma (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Public Feuds & Pain**: Surgical patient Lesya Yershova publicly revealed that she had suffered agonizing pain during her televised operation without anesthesia. Kashpirovsky also engaged in a bitter feud with rival psychic [[allan-chumak]] over who appeared on television first (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
* **Orthodox Backlash**: Lobbying by the increasingly powerful Russian Orthodox Church contributed heavily to the cancelation of alternative healing broadcasts (source: A “Common Madness” TV Psychics and Hypnosis in the Soviet Union.md).
