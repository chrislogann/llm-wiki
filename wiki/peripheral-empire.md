# Peripheral Empire

**Summary**: The third phase of Soviet global expansion (1959–1980s), characterized by the establishment of non-contiguous client states and military bases across the developing world through "teleguided" coups, proxy forces, and military assistance.

**Sources**: `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Unlike the contiguous land empire rebuilt by Lenin and extended by Stalin into Eastern Europe, the "Peripheral Empire" represented the global projection of Soviet power beyond the Eurasian landmass from 1959 onward (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Using maritime airlift capabilities, substantial economic and military subsidies, and proxy expeditionary armies, Moscow established influence across Latin America, Africa, the Middle East, and Asia (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Key Theaters and Operational Methods

1. **Cuba as the Hub and Proxy Force**:
   - Following Fidel Castro's 1959 revolution and subsequent alignment with Moscow, Cuba became the indispensable forward operating base in the Western Hemisphere (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - In the 1970s, Cuban troops served as the primary combat proxy for Soviet strategy in Africa, deploying tens of thousands of soldiers to support the MPLA in Angola (Operation Carlota) and the Derg regime of Mengistu Haile Mariam in Ethiopia during the Ogaden War (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

2. **"Teleguided" Coups and Military Regimes**:
   - The Soviets provided intelligence, arms, and covert support to Marxist-Leninist military factions, establishing client states such as the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen (South Yemen), the Republic of the Congo (Brazzaville), and the Marxist government of Mozambique (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - In Central America and the Caribbean, the Sandinista revolution in Nicaragua (1979) and Maurice Bishop's New Jewel Movement in Grenada (1979) expanded Soviet-Cuban influence (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

3. **Strategic Overextension and Rollback**:
   - The economic burden of subsidizing unproductive client states (Cuba alone cost billions of dollars annually in Soviet oil subsidies and sugar purchases) severely strained the Soviet domestic economy (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - **Operation Urgent Fury (Grenada, 1983)**: The U.S. military intervention following internal communist factional killings in Grenada constituted the first permanent territorial rollback of the peripheral empire (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
   - Under Mikhail Gorbachev's "New Thinking," Soviet financial subsidies and military support to overseas clients were drastically curtailed, leading to the collapse or ideological repositioning of peripheral regimes between 1988 and 1991 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
