# Dust Bowl and American Behemoth Comparison

**Summary**: A comparative analysis of Donald Worster's *Dust Bowl* and Michael Joseph Roberto's *Coming of the American Behemoth*, exploring their parallel critiques of the 1920s boom, corporate ideology, and New Deal interventions.

**Sources**: [Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md], [Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

In *Dust Bowl*, Donald Worster explores the ecological collapse of the Great Plains, while in *Coming of the American Behemoth*, Michael Joseph Roberto explores the socio-political drift toward fascism in the United States. Despite their different fields (environmental history vs. Marxist political history), both authors focus on the same historical era (1920–1940) and trace the crises of the 1930s back to the structural dynamics of American capitalism.

## The 1920s Roots of Crisis
Both authors argue that the crises of the 1930s (the Dust Bowl and the threat of corporate fascism) were not sudden aberrations, but were structurally prepared during the prosperous 1920s:
- **Monopoly Capital and Centralization**: Roberto argues that the 1920s "Great Boom" saw a massive consolidation of monopoly-finance capital, which centralized power in corporate cartels and intensified the exploitation of industrial labor (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **Speculative Sodbusting**: Worster similarly traces the Dust Bowl to the 1920s agricultural boom, during which mechanized "sodbusting" transformed farming into a highly commercialized factory system (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). Eager for high profits, farmers and agricultural investors overextended operations onto marginal lands, ignoring the volatile ecosystem (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md).

## Ideological Deception
Both works examine the role of boosterism and media in manufacturing public consent for capitalist expansion:
- **The Spectacle of Prosperity**: Roberto details how corporate elites used advertising, public relations, and a "democracy of goods" to pacify class conflict and deify business leaders (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Gambler's Ethos**: Worster details how plains boosters, local newspapers, and land agents promoted the myth that "rain follows the plow," encouraging a gambler's mentality that treated nature as infinite capital and dismissed warnings of environmental limits (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md).

## Critiques of New Deal Stabilization
Worster and Roberto offer similar skepticism toward the New Deal's role in restructuring the American system:
- **State Monopoly Capitalism**: Roberto notes that contemporary 1930s critics viewed the New Deal not as a progressive revolution, but as a form of state-monopoly capitalism (resembling European corporatist models) designed to rescue big business and stabilize the dictatorship of capital (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **Subsidizing Ecological Exploitation**: Worster critiques the New Deal's agricultural programs (like cattle buyouts in [[cimarron-county|Cimarron County]] and relief subsidies in [[haskell-county|Haskell County]]) as a state-sponsored safety net that absorbed the environmental risks of commercial farming without reforming its core exploitative values, thereby enabling the capitalist model to persist on the plains (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md).
