# Kansas Impeachment of 1862

**Summary**: The first impeachment proceeding in Kansas history, arising from a Civil War bond-sale scandal and fueled by the bitter factional warfare between Charles Robinson and James H. Lane.

**Sources**: [The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

In 1862, the young State of Kansas underwent its first major constitutional crisis when three top executive officials were impeached by the legislature (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). 

## The Bond Scandal
To fund the state militia and security operations during the Civil War, the Kansas legislature authorized the sale of war bonds (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). The law stipulated that the bonds could not be sold below par value (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). Facing extreme difficulties finding buyers, Secretary of State J. W. Robinson and State Auditor George S. Hillyer collaborated with local agents and the Secretary of the Interior to sell seven percent state bonds at approximately 40 to 60 cents on the dollar, while officially reporting them as sold at par value (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). 

## Political Trial and Factionalism
The bond sale irregularities were discovered by the legislature and seized upon by the radical faction led by Senator [[james-h-lane|James H. Lane]] (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). Lane's supporters used the scandal to destroy the moderate faction of Governor [[charles-robinson|Charles Robinson]] (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). 

In February 1862, the Kansas House of Representatives voted to impeach Governor Robinson, Secretary of State J. W. Robinson, and Auditor Hillyer (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Verjuice and Verdicts
The impeachment trial took place before the Kansas Senate in June 1862 (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md):
- **J. W. Robinson (Secretary of State)**: Convicted of high misdemeanors in office and removed (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
- **George S. Hillyer (Auditor)**: Convicted and removed from office (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
- **Charles Robinson (Governor)**: Acquitted of all charges (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). The Senate found no evidence that the governor had direct knowledge of or personal involvement in the illegal transactions, voting nearly unanimously for his acquittal (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

Despite the governor's acquittal, the scandal destroyed the moderate faction's credibility and secured the dominance of the Lane faction in subsequent state elections (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
