# Military Impunity in Russia

**Summary**: The systemic lack of accountability, institutional corruption, and protection from prosecution that characterizes the Russian Armed Forces and security services, particularly during the Chechen wars.

**Sources**: `[[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md`, `Chechnya_9780330350754.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Under President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]], the Russian military and security services have operated with a high degree of **military impunity**, shielded from independent judicial oversight and public accountability (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). This institutional culture has had severe consequences for both Russian conscripts and civilians in conflict zones (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## Internal Abuse of Conscripts
The lack of accountability begins within the barracks. Investigative journalist [[anna-politkovskaya|Anna Politkovskaya]] documented systemic abuses against conscripts, including:
- **Dedovshchina**: The hazing and torture of junior conscripts by senior soldiers and officers, often resulting in suicide or permanent injury (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).
- **Forced Labor**: Officers routinely "leasing out" or selling conscript soldiers as unpaid agricultural or construction laborers to private employers, pocketing the proceeds (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).
- **Subservient Military Courts**: Cases of abuse are investigated by military prosecutors and judged by military courts, whose officials are themselves active-duty officers loyal to the Ministry of Defense, resulting in systemic acquittals (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## War Crimes in [[chechnya|Chechnya]]
During the [[first-chechen-war|First]] and [[second-chechen-war|Second Chechen Wars]], Russian federal forces committed extensive human rights abuses against Chechen civilians, including torture, extrajudicial executions, and looting (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). 

Under the guise of the "counter-terrorist operation," the Russian leadership blocked investigations into these crimes. A prominent example was the **Dai Massacre** (or [[yury-budanov|Budanov]] Case II) in January 2002, where a GRU special operations unit executed six civilians in a minibus and burned their bodies (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md). Despite clear evidence, the military command transferred the accused out of [[chechnya|Chechnya]], and the central military prosecutor's office allowed their detention to expire, effectively letting the killers escape justice (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).

## Political Purpose
Impunity is not merely a failure of the system but a deliberate political choice. By protecting military personnel from prosecution, the Kremlin maintains the loyalty of the armed forces and security services, ensuring their willingness to execute harsh policies at home and abroad (source: [[vladimir-putin|Putin]]'s_Russia_-_life_in_a_failing_democracy_9780805082500.md).
