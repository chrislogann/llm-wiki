# Kursk Submarine Disaster

**Summary**: The August 2000 sinking of the Russian nuclear submarine *Kursk* in the Barents Sea, which resulted in 118 deaths and triggered severe political criticism of President Vladimir Putin's delayed response.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-16

---

The **Kursk Submarine Disaster** occurred on August 12, 2000, when the Oscar II-class Russian nuclear submarine *Kursk* sank during a large-scale naval training exercise in the Barents Sea, killing all 118 officers and crew members on board.

## Sinking and Torpedo Blast
During the exercise, a misfiring torpedo triggered a moderate blast in the bow, starting a fire. Two minutes and fifteen seconds later, a massive secondary explosion occurred (equivalent to a seismic event, detected by seismic stations as far away as Alaska and NATO submarines). The explosions sent the *Kursk* to the bottom of the Barents Sea, 354 feet below the surface (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

Most of the crew died instantly, but a group of **23 survivors** managed to barricade themselves in a rear compartment (Compartment 9) under Captain-Lieutenant Dmitri Kolesnikov. They waited in the cold and dark, hoping for rescue. Kolesnikov wrote a final note to his wife and commanders, dated August 12 at 3:15 PM (nearly eight hours after the explosion), which was later recovered (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Secrecy and Rescue Failures
The Russian military, led by Defense Minister Marshall Igor Sergeyev, initially covered up the disaster, claiming the training exercise was a success (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Later, officials falsely blamed the sinking on a collision with a NATO/U.S. submarine. 

The military rejected offers of international rescue assistance from Britain, Norway, Sweden, and the U.S. to protect nuclear secrets (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Putin, who was on vacation in Sochi and kept in the dark about the severity of the crisis, carried on jet-skiing and remained silent for days (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

On the sixth day of the crisis, the newspaper *Komsomolskaya Pravda* published the crew manifest after paying a $600 bribe to a military official, confirming the names of the 118 sailors (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

Putin finally accepted international assistance after a phone call from U.S. President Bill Clinton (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). On August 21, British and Norwegian divers opened the escape hatch in just six hours (a task the Russian Navy failed to achieve in nine days), finding all survivors dead.

## Vidyayevo Meeting and Political Impact
On August 22, Putin traveled to the closed garrison town of Vidyayevo to meet the grieving and angry families. In a tense and televised confrontation at the officers' club, Deputy PM Ilya Klebanov was shouted at by a mother, Nadezhda Tylik, who was subsequently sedated with a syringe on camera (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

The independent media, including ORT and NTV, heavily criticized the government's secrecy and incompetence (with headlines like "They don't sink" mocking the leadership) (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). This severe public backlash hardened Putin's resolve to bring national television channels under tight state control, eventually leading to the takeovers of both Berezovsky's and Gusinsky's media empires.

Instead of firing the commanders responsible (which would look like yielding to media pressure), Putin kept the hamstrung leadership politically dependent on him, while boosting military salaries to rebuild support (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
