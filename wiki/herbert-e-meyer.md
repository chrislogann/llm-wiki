**Herbert E. Meyer** was a key intelligence strategist in the United States during the late Cold War. Serving as the Vice Chairman of the National Intelligence Council (NIC) under Director of Central Intelligence William J. Casey and President Ronald Reagan, Meyer was instrumental in shifting US intelligence focus toward the internal vulnerabilities of the Soviet Union (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).

## Analytical Philosophy and Contributions

Meyer is best known for his contrarian analytical assessments that challenged the prevailing CIA consensus that the Soviet Union was a permanent, stable global power. 

- **Predicting Soviet Collapse**: In 1983, Meyer authored a series of influential memoranda arguing that the Soviet Union was entering its "final pages" due to deep economic stagnation, nationality conflicts, and demographic decline (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).
- **Correlation of Forces**: He argued that aggressive US policies—such as increased defense spending, technological crackdowns, and support for anti-communist insurgencies—were successfully shifting the balance of power back to the West.
- **Support for the Reagan Doctrine**: His assessments provided the intellectual justification for the [[reagan-doctrine]], supporting rollback strategies rather than containment.

## Legacy
While some contemporary critics viewed his assessments as overly speculative or politicized, Meyer's forecasts regarding the structural collapse of the Soviet state within a twenty-year window proved highly prescient, aligning closely with the dissolution of the USSR in 1991.
