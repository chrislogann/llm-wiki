# Allamvedelmi Hatosag

**Summary**: The hated State Security Authority (AVH/AVO) of Hungary, which used torture, networks of informers, and terror to sustain [[matyas-rakosi|Matyas Rakosi]]'s Stalinist dictatorship, dissolved during the 1956 Revolution.

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **Allamvedelmi Hatosag** (AVH, State Security Authority, commonly referred to by its earlier acronym **AVO**) was the secret police of Hungary from 1945 to 1956 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Modeled directly on the Soviet NKVD/KGB, it was the primary instrument of terror used by dictator [[matyas-rakosi|Matyas Rakosi]] to enforce Stalinist rule (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Structure and Operations
Under its chief, Gabor Peter (a former tailor’s assistant and Soviet KGB agent), the AVH constructed a pervasive police state (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Headquartered at 60 Stalin Avenue in Budapest, the AVH employed over 48,000 officers and maintained a network of hundreds of thousands of informers (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). 

Its methods included:
- **"Bell Fever" (csengőfrász)**: The terror of nighttime arrests, where individuals were dragged from their homes to be interned or interrogated without trial (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Brutal Torture**: The basement of 60 Stalin Avenue was equipped with torture chambers, featuring whips, nails, and acid baths (lefolyó) to dissolve victims' remains into the city sewage system (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Interrogators like Gyula Prinz used techniques like the "short iron" and "wolf's bandage" to extract confessions (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Political Show Trials**: The AVH manufactured evidence to eliminate [[matyas-rakosi|Rakosi]]'s political rivals, notably organizing the torture and execution of Laszlo Rajk in 1949 and church leaders like Cardinal Jozsef Mindszenty (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Role in the 1956 Uprising
The AVH was the primary target of public hatred during the [[hungarian-revolution-1956|1956 Revolution]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). The revolution began on October 23 when AVH guards fired on unarmed student demonstrators outside the Radio Station (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). 

During the street fighting, AVH officers were hunted down by revolutionaries (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Many captured AVH officers were lynched, beaten to death, or hung from trees by angry crowds in the streets of Budapest, their bodies covered in lime (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Prime Minister [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]] officially abolished the AVH on October 28 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Re-establishment
Following the suppression of the revolution, [[janos-kadar|Janos Kadar]]'s regime did not officially reconstitute the AVH under its old name, instead integrating its functions into the Ministry of Internal Affairs as a restructured intelligence service to avoid the public stigma of [[matyas-rakosi|Rakosi]]'s terror (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
