# Josip Broz Tito

**Summary**: The nationalist and Communist leader of Yugoslavia from 1945 to 1980, who led the Partisan resistance in WWII, successfully defied Stalin's attempts to subordinate his nation, and pioneered the Cold War policy of non-alignment.

**Sources**: `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Josip Broz Tito (1892–1980), born Josip Broz, was a Croat Communist who served in the Austro-Hungarian Army during World War I and was captured by Russian forces in 1915 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Between 1917 and 1923, he fought in [[vladimir-lenin|Vladimir Lenin]]'s Red Army before returning to Yugoslavia, where he rose to lead the illegal Communist Party and was imprisoned for six years in 1928 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). In 1936, the [[comintern|Comintern]] sent Tito to Paris to manage the logistical and travel arrangements for the International Brigades fighting in the [[spanish-civil-war|Spanish Civil War]] (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## World War II and the Partisan Resistance
Following the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in April 1941, Tito remained inactive in compliance with the Nazi-Soviet Pact until Germany invaded the USSR in June 1941 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). He then mobilized the Communist Partisans into a highly effective guerrilla army to fight the German occupiers and rival royalist forces led by [[drazha-mihailovich|Drazha Mihailovich]] (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). 

Tito successfully secured sole Allied military support by deceiving British Prime Minister Winston Churchill. In their meeting in Naples on August 12, 1944, Tito falsely assured Churchill that he had no desire to introduce a Communist system in postwar Yugoslavia (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Supported by a Soviet disinformation campaign led by mole [[james-klugmann|James Klugmann]] within the British SOE, Tito discredited Mihailovich and positioned his Partisans to enter Belgrade in triumph with the Red Army on October 20, 1944 (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## The Split with Stalin
On March 7, 1945, Tito became the Prime Minister and commander-in-chief of a new Communist government in Yugoslavia (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Because the Partisans had liberated most of Yugoslavia independently rather than relying on Red Army occupation, Tito held significant nationalist leverage (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). 

In 1948, Tito defied Stalin's attempts to subordinate Yugoslavian economic and foreign policy to Moscow's control, resulting in Yugoslavia's expulsion from the Cominform and multiple failed Soviet assassination plots (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Defying the Kremlin, Tito successfully negotiated Western economic and military aid and established Yugoslavia as a neutral, "non-aligned" state, making it the first successful exception to Soviet imperial satellization (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
