# [[alger-hiss|Alger Hiss]]

**Summary**: A high-ranking US State Department official who served as a key source for a Soviet espionage network in the 1930s and was convicted of perjury in 1950 after being exposed by [[whittaker-chambers|Whittaker Chambers]].

**Sources**: `Witness_9780895267894.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

[[alger-hiss|Alger Hiss]] (1904–1996) was a prominent American lawyer and diplomat (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). A graduate of Harvard Law School, he served as a law clerk to Supreme Court Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. before entering government service during the New Deal (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Government Career and Infiltration
Hiss joined the Agricultural Adjustment Administration (AAA) in 1933, where he became a member of the Communist **[[ware-group|Ware Group]]** (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). He later moved to the State Department, serving as an assistant to Assistant Secretary of State Francis Sayre (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

Hiss acted as the No. 1 source in the State Department for the Soviet military intelligence (GRU) network run by [[whittaker-chambers|Whittaker Chambers]], copying and typing secret State Department documents to be transmitted to Moscow (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). He rose to high prominence within the department, serving as a member of the US delegation to the 1945 Yalta Conference and as the temporary Secretary-General of the United Nations charter conference in San Francisco (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Exposure and Conviction
In August 1948, [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] publicly identified Hiss as a Communist agent before the House Un-American Activities Committee (HUAC) (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Hiss adamantly denied the allegations under oath, claiming he did not even know [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). 

The case escalated when [[whittaker-chambers|Chambers]] produced microfilm and handwritten summaries in Hiss’s handwriting (the "Pumpkin Papers") that matched documents Hiss had copied from the State Department (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Because the statute of limitations for espionage had expired, Hiss was indicted for perjury (source: Witness_9780895267894.md). Following a hung jury in his first trial, Hiss was convicted in his second trial on January 21, 1950, and sentenced to five years in federal prison (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).

## Post-Cold War Evidence
Though Hiss maintained his innocence until his death, subsequent historical releases have confirmed his guilt (source: Witness_9780895267894.md):
- **The Venona Project**: Declassified Soviet intelligence cables decrypted by the United States pointed to a GRU agent codenamed "ALES" who was active in the State Department and attended the Yalta Conference, matching only Hiss (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).
- **Hungarian Archives**: A 1954 interrogation dossier of American Communist Noel Field, released from Hungarian Interior Ministry archives in 1993, explicitly named Hiss as his partner in Soviet espionage (source: Witness_9780895267894.md).
