# Ragnarok

**Summary**: The apocalyptic judgment day in Norse mythology, preceded by a bitter winter, which Nazi propaganda used to evoke geographic unity and a call to arms for Northern European nations.

**Sources**: `Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Ragnarok is the cataclysmic "judgment day" in Norse mythology, characterized by great battles, the death of major gods (including Odin), and the engulfment of the world by the sea, before a new age of peace arises under Odin's son Balder (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). It is preceded by a period of bitter winter and intense warfare (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).

## Propaganda Co-optation

Nazi propaganda posters leveraged elements of the Ragnarok narrative, particularly the concept of a looming, apocalyptic conflict:
- **Nordic Alliance**: Posters depicted cold, snowy mountain peaks representing the shared environment of Norway, Finland, and Nazi Germany (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). 
- **Call to Action**: One poster showed three swords representing the three nations raised against a starry sky and icy peaks, invoking a shared Norse heritage in a fight "for freedom" (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). The combined imagery was meant to suggest that, just as the gods united to fight the forces of evil at Ragnarok, these nations had to stand together in the cold winter of war to combat the Soviet threat (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
