# Charles Robinson

**Summary**: The first Governor of the State of Kansas (1861–1863) and leader of the moderate, legalistic faction of the Free-State party.

**Sources**: [The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Charles Robinson was a pivotal figure in the establishment of the Kansas state government (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). Calm, deliberate, and business-oriented, he advocated for legal and constitutional methods to resolve territorial disputes, in contrast to the radical and militaristic factions of the Free-State party (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Governorship and Factional Feud
Robinson took office in early 1861, just as Kansas was admitted to the Union and the Civil War commenced (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). His administration was immediately paralyzed by a bitter factional feud with General [[james-h-lane|James H. Lane]], who served as one of the state's first U.S. Senators (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). Lane used his close relationship with President Abraham Lincoln to seize control of military patronage in Kansas, undermining Robinson’s authority over the state militia and recruitment (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Impeachment Trial of 1862
The Robinson-Lane feud culminated in the [[kansas-impeachment-1862|Kansas Impeachment of 1862]] (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). Robinson was impeached by the Kansas House of Representatives for his role in the irregular sale of state war bonds (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). During the trial in the Senate, prosecutors failed to prove that Robinson had participated in or benefited from the illegal sale of bonds below par value, and he was acquitted on all counts (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). However, the trial successfully tarnished his political reputation, and he was defeated in the 1862 gubernatorial election (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
