# The birth of Kansas

**Summary**: G. Raymond Gaeddert's political history detailing the establishment of the Kansas state government from 1859 to 1863, focusing on factional conflicts, constitutional conventions, and early administration crises.

**Sources**: [The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

In *The birth of Kansas*, G. Raymond Gaeddert examines the political and economic struggles surrounding the establishment of the Kansas state government (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). The book moves past the early territorial battles to focus on the period between 1859 and 1863, analyzing how factional warfare within the dominant Free-State party shaped the new state's institutions and governance (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Free-State Factionalism
With the territorial question settled in favor of a free state, the Free-State party fractured into two opposing factions led by the state's most prominent politicians (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md):
- **Robinson Faction**: Led by Governor [[charles-robinson|Charles Robinson]], representing a moderate, legalistic approach that favored constitutional process, diplomacy, and business-minded governance (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
- **Lane Faction**: Led by General [[james-h-lane|James H. Lane]], representing a radical, impatient, and militaristic faction that prioritized armed action and opportunistic political maneuvering (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

This bitter internal rivalry dominated the Wyandotte Constitutional Convention, the election of early state officers, and the first years of statehood (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Constitutional Foundations
The book documents the drafting and ratification of the [[wyandotte-constitution|Wyandotte Constitution]] in 1859 (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). Despite political maneuvering and debates over boundaries, suffrage, and apportionment, the convention succeeded in establishing a free-state framework that paved the way for Kansas's admission to the Union in early 1861 (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).

## Early Administration and Impeachment
The outbreak of the Civil War coincided with the birth of the state, placing extreme financial and military strains on the Robinson administration (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). The struggle between Lane and Robinson escalated to a national level when Lane was elected to the U.S. Senate and gained patronage control (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). 

This rivalry culminated in the [[kansas-impeachment-1862|Kansas Impeachment of 1862]], in which Robinson and other state officers were tried by the legislature for the irregular sale of state war bonds below par value (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md). The trial resulted in the acquittal of Governor Robinson but the conviction and removal of the Secretary of State J. W. Robinson and State Auditor George S. Hillyer (source: The_birth_of_Kansas_9780879913359.md).
