# [[georgian-affair|Georgian Affair]]

**Summary**: A political conflict in 1922-1923 within the leadership of the Soviet Union regarding the incorporation of the Georgian Soviet Socialist Republic into the USSR, which pitted Vladimir Lenin and Leon Trotsky against Joseph Stalin's centralizing national policy.

**Sources**: `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **[[georgian-affair|Georgian Affair]]** (also known as the Georgian Case or Georgian Question) was a critical political clash inside the Bolshevik leadership during 1922–1923 (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). The dispute centered on how Georgia should be integrated into the Soviet Union: either as a direct member state with some local autonomy (as favored by Lenin and the Georgian Bolsheviks) or subsumed under a Transcaucasian Federation dominated by Moscow (as driven by Stalin) (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## Stalin's Bureaucratic Coup
In early 1923, [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]], acting behind [[vladimir-lenin|Lenin]]'s back, utilized Sergo Ordzhonikidze and Felix Dzerzhinsky to carry out an organized political coup against the Georgian Bolshevik leadership (including Polikarp Mdivani and Mikhail Okudzhava) (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). Ordzhonikidze used physical violence and heavy-handed administrative methods to enforce Moscow's centralist policy, which Stalin and Dzerzhinsky defended and shielded from criticism (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## Lenin's "Bomb" against Stalin
Lenin, though incapacitated by illness, was deeply alarmed by Stalin's personal disloyalty and his chauvinistic policy on the national question (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). He termed the bureaucratic degeneration of the party a "most dangerous ulcer" and began preparing a political "bomb" to expose Stalin at the Twelfth Party Congress (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md):
- **Appeal to Trotsky**: On March 5, 1923, Lenin sent a direct note to [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]] asking him to take up the defense of the Georgian Bolsheviks in the Central Committee: *"Dear Comrade Trotsky: I wish very much to ask you to take upon yourself the defense of the Georgian case... At present, the case is under the 'persecution' of Stalin and Dzerzhinsky, and I cannot trust their impartiality"* (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Breaking Relations with Stalin**: Lenin's wife, Nadezhda Krupskaya, revealed to Kamenev that Lenin had dictated a letter to Stalin breaking off all personal relations with him, both due to Stalin's rudeness to Krupskaya and to crush him politically (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Message to the Georgians**: Lenin sent a supportive note to Mdivani and other Georgian opponents of Stalin: *"I am watching your case with all my heart and soul. Ordzhonikidze’s rough methods and Stalin’s and Dzerzhinsky’s encouragement fill me with indignation"* (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

## Trotsky's Proposed Compromise and the Triumvirate's Betrayal
To prevent a destructive split at the party congress, Trotsky proposed a compromise to Lev Kamenev, suggesting they preserve the organizational *status quo* (retaining Stalin, Dzerzhinsky, and Ordzhonikidze in their posts) on the condition of an immediate change in national policy, an end to the persecution of Georgian opponents, and Stalin apologizing to Krupskaya (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). 

Stalin initially accepted these terms (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). However, when Lenin suffered another stroke that left him permanently unable to speak or write, the triumvirate (Stalin, Zinoviev, and Kamenev) reneged on the agreement (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). Kamenev traveled to Tiflis (Tbilisi) and carried out Stalin’s centralizing policy at the Georgian party conference, sealing the consolidation of the Stalinist faction (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
