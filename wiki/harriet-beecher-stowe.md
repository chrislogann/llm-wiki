# Harriet Beecher Stowe

**Summary**: An American author and abolitionist (1811–1896) best known for her landmark anti-slavery novel *Uncle Tom's Cabin*.

**Sources**: [The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Harriet Beecher Stowe was a prominent nineteenth-century American writer and social reformer whose work played a significant role in shape-shifting public sentiment against slavery (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). Born in Litchfield, Connecticut, into a highly religious and intellectual family (her father was Calvinist preacher Lyman Beecher), Stowe grew up in an environment focused on theological debates and social issues (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).

## Move to Cincinnati and Exposure to Slavery
In 1832, Stowe moved to Cincinnati, Ohio, where her father became president of the Lane Theological Seminary (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). Living just across the Ohio River from Kentucky, a slave state, Stowe witnessed firsthand the operations of the Underground Railroad and heard the stories of runaway slaves (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). These experiences deeply affected her and provided the raw material for her later writings (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).

## The Writing of *Uncle Tom's Cabin*
Following the passage of the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850, which legally mandated that citizens in free states assist in the recapture of runaway slaves, Stowe resolved to write an anti-slavery novel to mobilize northern public opinion (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). The resulting work, *Uncle Tom's Cabin*, serialized in 1851 and published as a book in 1852, brought the human suffering of enslaved people into the homes of millions of readers (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).

## Legacy and Lincoln Meeting
Stowe's novel made her an international celebrity and a hero to the abolitionist cause (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). Her writing was so politically potent that when she met President Abraham Lincoln in 1862 during the Civil War, he is famously reported to have greeted her as, "So you're the little woman who wrote the book that made this great war!" (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).
