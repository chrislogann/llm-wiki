# [[first-indochina-war|First Indochina War]]

**Summary**: The conflict from 1946 to 1954 between French colonial forces and the communist-led Viet Minh nationalist movement, culminating in the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu and the partitioning of Vietnam.

**Sources**: `Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **[[first-indochina-war|First Indochina War]]** (also known as the French Indochina War) began in December 1946 when tensions between the French colonial administration and the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV) under [[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]] erupted into open warfare in Hanoi (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Outbreak of Conflict
Following WWII, France sought to re-establish control over its colonies in Indochina (Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia) (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). Although the French initially recognized the DRV as a "free state" within the French Union, negotiations broke down over the status of southern Vietnam (Cochinchina) (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

In November 1946, French warships bombarded the port city of Haiphong, killing thousands of civilians, and by December, Viet Minh forces launched a coordinated retaliatory attack on French positions in Hanoi, initiating the war (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Geopolitical Escalation
Initially a colonial struggle, the war was quickly integrated into the global Cold War (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). Following the communist victory in China in 1949, Mao Zedong's regime began providing the Viet Minh with heavy weapons, training, and sanctuary (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

Fearing the spread of communism in Southeast Asia under the "domino theory," the United States began heavily financing the French war effort (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). By 1954, the US was paying nearly 80 percent of the French war costs (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Military Campaigns and the French Defeat
French commanders struggled to combat the guerrilla tactics and growing conventional strength of the Viet Minh under General [[vo-nguyen-giap|Vo Nguyen Giap]] (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). The French army sought to lure the Viet Minh into a set-piece battle to destroy them with superior firepower (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

To do so, they established a fortified base in the remote valley of Dien Bien Phu (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). However, during the **[[battle-of-dien-bien-phu|Battle of Dien Bien Phu]]** (March–May 1954), Giap surrounded and destroyed the French garrison, shattering French political will to continue the war (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Aftermath
The war officially ended with the signing of the **[[geneva-accords-1954|Geneva Accords]]** in July 1954, which temporarily partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel and granted independence to Laos and Cambodia (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).
