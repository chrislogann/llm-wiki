# The Annotated Uncle Tom's Cabin

**Summary**: The annotated edition of Harriet Beecher Stowe's influential 1852 anti-slavery novel, which fueled the abolitionist movement in the pre-Civil War United States.

**Sources**: [The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

*Uncle Tom's Cabin*, written by [[harriet-beecher-stowe|Harriet Beecher Stowe]] and published in 1852, is one of the most politically and socially significant novels in American history (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). The novel dramatizes the cruelties and moral contradictions of chattel slavery through the narrative of Uncle Tom, an enslaved man whose Christian faith and resilience remain steadfast in the face of brutal oppression (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).

## Historical Context and Themes
Written in the wake of the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850, Stowe designed the novel to force northern readers to confront the moral evils of slavery (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). Key themes include:
- **The Incompatibility of Slavery and Christianity**: Stowe contrasts Christian charity and love with the legalistic, dehumanizing structures of the slave trade (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).
- **The Evils of Sectional Compromise**: The novel highlights how northern apathy and legal compliance with southern slaveholder demands implicated the entire nation in the moral sin of slavery (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).
- **The Economic and Structural Critique (Chapter 19)**: Through the character of Augustine St. Clare, the novel articulates a structural critique of slavery that draws parallels between Southern slaveholders and Northern or English industrial capitalists, pointing out that both systems are based on labor exploitation (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).

## Reception and Impact
*Uncle Tom's Cabin* was an immediate sensation, becoming the best-selling novel of the nineteenth century and second only to the Bible in total sales (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md). It galvanized the northern abolitionist movement, helping to mobilize public opinion against slavery and directly fueling the sectional tensions that led to the American Civil War (source: The_annotated_Uncle_Tom's_cabin_9780393059465.md).
