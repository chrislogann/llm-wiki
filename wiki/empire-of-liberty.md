# Empire of Liberty

**Summary**: The universalist ideology driving United States foreign policy and interventionism, centering on individual liberty, private property, free market capitalism, and a teleological belief in the global applicability of the American model.

**Sources**: `Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The concept of the "Empire of Liberty" (originating with Thomas Jefferson) represents the ideological foundation of American expansionism and Cold War interventionism (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). US policymakers viewed their political and economic system not as a local development, but as a universal blueprint for the future of all societies (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Core Pillars of the Ideology

### 1. Liberty and Private Property
According to the Jeffersonian view, true liberty is inextricably linked to private property (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Only individuals with property possess the independence required to be responsible citizens of a republic, which historically led to the exclusion of non-property holders and colonized populations from the "circle of enlightenment" (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 2. Anti-Collectivism and Suspicion of State Power
The ideology is characterized by a strong distrust of centralized political power and state-led planning (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Collectivism is viewed as the ultimate negation of liberty, making socialist models or state-driven reforms in the Third World targets of intense suspicion and hostility (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 3. Science, Technology, and Modernity
Under the American model, science and technology are viewed as neutral tools for rational development (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Modernization is equated with replicating the American industrial and social model, demanding that traditional societies discard historical or local structures to achieve progress (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 4. The Free Market
Free enterprise and commerce are seen as both the engine of prosperity and a primary guarantor of individual freedom (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Protecting global shipping lanes, securing resource access, and opening foreign markets are framed as moral duties to expand the sphere of liberty (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Cold War Application
During the Cold War, the "Empire of Liberty" drove the United States to contain communism globally, frequently justifying interventions against popular movements or sovereign governments in the Third World under the guise of preventing collectivist domination and promoting modernization (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
