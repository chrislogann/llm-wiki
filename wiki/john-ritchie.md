# John Ritchie

**Summary**: An early Topeka pioneer, radical abolitionist, G.A.R. veteran, and philanthropist who operated an Underground Railroad depot and donated land to establish Washburn College.

**Sources**: [Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Col. John Ritchie was a key figure in the Free-State movement and early development of Topeka, Kansas (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Coming to Topeka on April 3, 1855, Ritchie established himself as a farmer and built the Ritchie Block, the first brick building in the city (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## Radical Abolitionism and John Brown
Ritchie was a passionate abolitionist and a supporter of General [[james-h-lane|James H. Lane]] (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). He was a close friend and admirer of John Brown, and it was at Ritchie's house that John Brown spent his last night in Kansas (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Ritchie operated a major depot on the Underground Railroad, helping secure the passage of escaping slaves (estimating that over $100,000 worth of runaway slaves passed safely through his property) (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). 

## Defense Against Pro-Slavery Marshals
In the late 1850s, a pro-slavery deputy marshal named Arms attempted to arrest Ritchie on a warrant from the Pro-Slavery legislature at Lecompton (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Ritchie refused to submit to the "bogus" authority. When Arms drew his weapon, Ritchie shot and killed him in self-defense (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Ritchie surrendered to local authorities and was honorably acquitted; the incident permanently ended the serving of Lecompton warrants and harassment of Topeka citizens by pro-slavery marshals (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## Constitutional Conventions and Civil War Service
Ritchie was a delegate to both the Leavenworth and [[wyandotte-constitution|Wyandotte]] constitutional conventions (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). During the Civil War, he enlisted as a private in Company A, Fifth Kansas Cavalry (Lane's Guards) and was promoted to captain (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). He was subsequently commissioned to raise and command an Indian regiment as Colonel, and was breveted brigadier general at the war's conclusion (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## Founding of Washburn College
In 1857, Ritchie was appointed to the Congregational committee tasked with locating a Christian college in Kansas (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). In 1865, he served as a founding trustee of Lincoln College (later [[washburn-college|Washburn College]]) (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Ritchie made the school's most significant initial donation, contributing $3,000 and deeding 160 acres of land which became the permanent campus of the college (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).
