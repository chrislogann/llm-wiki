# Mein Kampf

**Summary**: Adolf Hitler's autobiographical manifesto, which outlines the core tactical principles of Nazi propaganda, including the prioritization of emotion, anti-intellectualism, and the utility of the "Big Lie."

**Sources**: `Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

In *Mein Kampf*, Adolf Hitler details his views on political organization and propaganda, drawing inspiration from British WWI propaganda and the demagogic methods of Karl Lueger in Vienna (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md). For Hitler, propaganda is a critical tool for gaining power, which must be judged solely by its success and not by moral standards (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md).

## Core Propaganda Principles in the Text

1. **Emotional Appeal**: Hitler asserts that propaganda must always address the masses rather than the intelligentsia (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md). It must bypass logical reason and appeal directly to primitive feelings, instincts, and emotions (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md).
2. **Anti-Intellectualism**: The text displays deep contempt for the intellect, arguing that the "heart and virtues" are far more valuable to a political movement than "pale external knowledge" (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md). Hitler characterizes the masses as forgetful, lazy, and feminized, requiring simple, repetitious slogans rather than intellectual explanations (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md).
3. **The Big Lie (*Meisterlüge*)**: Hitler famously writes that the masses fall victim to a "big lie" more easily than a small one, as they themselves might tell small lies but would be too ashamed to fabricate a falsehood of massive proportions (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md). Even when exposed, a sufficiently grand lie will leave some of its influence behind (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md).
4. **Single Category of Foe**: To prevent doubt among followers, the leader must group all different opponents into a single, unified category of enemy (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md).
5. **No Discussion**: Hitler advocates for absolute authority and the strict prohibition of debates or opposing viewpoints in rallies, using force to secure the speaker's platform (source: Propaganda_als_Waffe_9783752659368.md).
