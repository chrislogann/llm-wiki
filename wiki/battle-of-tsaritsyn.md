# [[battle-of-tsaritsyn|Battle of Tsaritsyn]]

**Summary**: A major battleground during the [[russian-civil-war|Russian Civil War]] (1918–1920) for control of the strategic city of Tsaritsyn, which became the crucible for the "Tsaritsyn group" of Soviet commanders and a key battleground in the political feud between [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]] and [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]].

**Sources**: `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`, `1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md`, `My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md`

The **[[battle-of-tsaritsyn|Battle of Tsaritsyn]]** was fought from 1918 to 1920 during the [[russian-civil-war|Russian Civil War]] for control of the strategic Volga port city of Tsaritsyn (later renamed Stalingrad, now Volgograd) (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). The city was contested between the Bolshevik Red Army and the anti-communist White armies (the Don Cossacks and Denikin's Armed Forces of Southern Russia) (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## Geopolitical and Strategic Context
Tsaritsyn served as a critical transit hub for grain and food supplies transported from the northern Caucasus and lower Volga to feed the starving populations of Bolshevik-controlled Moscow and Petrograd (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). For the White forces, capturing Tsaritsyn was necessary to establish a physical link between General Anton Denikin's southern Volunteer Army and Admiral Aleksandr Kolchak's eastern forces advancing from Siberia (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

## Key Phases of the Conflict

### 1. The 1918 Sieges and the Stalin-Trotsky Feud
The first sieges were launched by Pyotr Krasnov’s Don Cossacks in 1918 (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). General Konstantin Mamontov’s cavalry was repulsed by the city's defenses (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). 

During the defense, [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]] was sent by [[vladimir-lenin|Lenin]] to Tsaritsyn to secure grain requisitions (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Stalin instead took control of the local Revolutionary Military Council and the Red 10th Army, acting as the de facto front commander (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). 

Here, Stalin formed his loyal inner circle of commanders (the **"Tsaritsyn Group"**, including Kliment Voroshilov, Semyon Budyonny, Semyon Timoshenko, and Grigory Kulik) who would later dominate the Soviet military (source: 1929_Sino-Soviet_War_-_The_War_Nobody_Knew_9780700623761.md). 

Tsaritsyn became the centerpiece of the Stalin-Trotsky feud (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Stalin vehemently opposed War Commissar [[leon-trotsky|Leon Trotsky]]'s recruitment of former tsarist military specialists, instead purging and arresting them in the city (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Stalin also expanded the Cheka in Tsaritsyn, ordering mass executions to enforce his control (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

### Trotsky's Account: Centralization vs. the "Tsaritsyn Opposition"
In his autobiography *My Life*, Trotsky details his struggle to bring the insubordinate Tsaritsyn command under centralized Soviet authority (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md):
- **Voroshilov's Irregulars**: Trotsky characterizes Voroshilov's circle as NCOs and irregular peasant fighters who lacked military training but vehemently opposed "specialists," military academy graduates, and commands from Moscow, raising cries of "treason" whenever their disproportionate demands for ammunition were denied (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Trotsky's Ultimatum**: Trotsky traveled to Tsaritsyn and threatened to send Voroshilov to Moscow under armed convoy to face a revolutionary tribunal unless he agreed to obey direct orders (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Stalin's Recall**: Frustrated by the division's ongoing insubordination, Trotsky categorically demanded Stalin's recall, sending a wire to Lenin on October 4, 1918: *"I insist categorically on Stalin’s recall. The Tsaritsin front is in a bad way... If this is not done tomorrow, I will commit Voroshilov to trial"* (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **The "Rotten Compromise"**: Sverdlov brought Stalin back to Moscow. On the train, Stalin pleaded: *"Do you really wish to dismiss them all? They are fine boys!"* to which Trotsky replied: *"Those fine boys will ruin the revolution"* (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md). In January 1919, after further compromise failed, Trotsky telegraphed Lenin: *"I consider Stalin’s patronage of the Tsaritsin policy a most dangerous ulcer, worse than any treason"* (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).
- **Foundation of the Regime**: Trotsky argues that Stalin systematically gathered the discontented military figures whose vanity had been trodden on in Tsaritsyn, turning them into the core political circle that eventually enabled Stalin's rise to absolute power (source: My_Life_-_An_Attempt_at_an_Autobiography_9780486123400.md).

### 2. The 1919 Fall to the Whites
In mid-June 1919, General Baron Pyotr Wrangel (commanding the Army of the Caucasus) launched a fresh siege and captured the city (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Wrangel's forces succeeded with British military assistance: the Royal Air Force's 47 Squadron (flying Sopwith Camels) strafed Budyonny's cavalry, and a single British Mark V tank (led by Major Ewen Cameron-Bruce) broke the Red trenches (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). 

Following the victory, Denikin issued the famous **"Moscow Directive"** on July 3, 1919, ordering a three-pronged advance to capture Moscow (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Wrangel strongly criticized the plan, arguing they should instead consolidate and capture Astrakhan (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).

### 3. Red Army Recapture (1920)
In late 1919, the overextended White advance on Moscow collapsed (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). Sergei Kamenev, appointed Commander-in-Chief of the Red Army by Stalin's faction, directed the offensive to retake Tsaritsyn (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md). On January 2, 1920, the Red 10th Army recaptured the city, cutting off the White armies in the Caucasus (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
