# Hungarian Revolution of 1956

**Summary**: A nationwide popular revolt against the Soviet-imposed Stalinist regime in Hungary, lasting [[twelve-days|twelve days]] before being bloodily suppressed by a massive Soviet military invasion.

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **Hungarian Revolution of 1956** was a pivotal event of the Cold War, demonstrating the limits of Soviet control in Eastern Europe and the reality of Western realpolitik (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Causes and Background
Following WWII, Hungary was integrated into the Soviet bloc under the repressive dictatorship of [[matyas-rakosi|Matyas Rakosi]], whose secret police, the [[allamvedelmi-hatosag|AVH]], arrested, tortured, and executed thousands of citizens (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Following Stalin’s death in 1953, the reformist [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]] served briefly as Prime Minister, introducing popular reforms before being ousted by [[matyas-rakosi|Rakosi]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). 

In 1956, Nikita Khrushchev’s Secret Speech denouncing Stalin and the reformist worker uprisings in Poznan, Poland, inspired Hungarian students and intellectuals to demand national sovereignty, free speech, and the return of [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Course of the Revolution (October 23 – November 4)

### October 23: The Spark
On October 23, 1956, a peaceful student march in Budapest swelled into a crowd of over 200,000 demonstrators demanding the **"Sixteen Points"** (national independence, withdrawal of Soviet troops, free elections, and [[imre-nagy|Nagy]]’s return) (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Protesters tore down the massive bronze statue of Stalin in City Park (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). That night, AVH guards fired on unarmed crowds gathered outside the Radio Station, sparking an armed rebellion as Hungarian soldiers gave their weapons to the protesters (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### The First Soviet Withdrawal
The Central Committee appointed [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]] as Prime Minister on October 24 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Soviet tanks initially entered Budapest, but met fierce resistance from young "freedom fighters" using petrol bombs (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). On October 28, [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] declared a ceasefire, abolished the AVH, and announced negotiations for a total Soviet military withdrawal (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). By October 30, Soviet troops began pulling out of Budapest (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

### The Declaration of Neutrality
Believing the crisis resolved and seeking to secure Hungary's independence, [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] formed a multi-party coalition government (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). On November 1, upon learning that new Soviet columns were entering the country, [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] declared Hungary's withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact and appealed to the United Nations to guarantee Hungarian neutrality (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## The Suez Crisis and Western Inaction
For years, the United States had encouraged uprisings against Soviet rule via Radio Free Europe, implying that Western military aid would support liberation (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). However, the revolution coincided exactly with the **Suez Crisis**, in which Britain, France, and Israel invaded Egypt (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Seeking to avoid nuclear war and distracted by the Middle East, US President Dwight D. Eisenhower decided to take no action, signaling to Moscow that the West would not intervene (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## November 4: Operation Whirlwind
Having secured Western inaction and fearing that Hungary's exit would dissolve the Warsaw Pact, Khrushchev ordered a massive assault under Marshal Ivan Konev (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Overnight on November 4, 17 Soviet divisions and over 60,000 troops invaded Budapest (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Despite desperate resistance, the revolution was crushed within a few days (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Aftermath
More than 2,500 Hungarians and 700 Soviet troops were killed, and over 200,000 Hungarians fled as refugees to the West (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). [[imre-nagy|Nagy]] was arrested, replaced by Soviet puppet [[janos-kadar|Janos Kadar]], and executed in 1958 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). The defeat secured Soviet hegemony over Central Europe for another three decades (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).
