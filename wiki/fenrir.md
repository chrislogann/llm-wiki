# Fenrir

**Summary**: A monstrous wolf in Norse mythology, son of Loki, whose legends of binding and role in Ragnarok were co-opted by Nazi propaganda to represent the threat of Soviet Bolshevism.

**Sources**: `Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

In Norse mythology, Fenrir is the monstrous wolf fathered by the trickster god Loki (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). 

## Mythological Background

- **The Binding of Fenrir**: Fenrir grew so large and fierce in Asgard that only the god Tyr dared to feed him (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). Fearing his strength, the gods had dwarfs forge a magical chain to bind him (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). Fenrir only allowed them to bind him on the condition that someone place a hand in his mouth as a pledge of good faith. Tyr bravely sacrificed his hand when the wolf realized he was trapped (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
- **Ragnarok**: During the final battle of [[ragnarok]], Fenrir breaks free, advancing with his jaws stretching from earth to sky, and devours Odin before being slain by Odin's son Vidar (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).

## Use in Nazi Propaganda

Nazi propaganda posters utilized the imagery of a Norse shield and sword combating a red wolf to warn the Scandinavian public of the Soviet threat to the East (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). By drawing parallels to Fenrir, the propaganda sought to convince citizens that the Soviets were a grave, monstrous threat that must be forcefully repressed, similar to the binding of Fenrir (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
