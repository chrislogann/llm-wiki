# Imre Nagy

**Summary**: The reformist Communist Prime Minister of Hungary who led the 1956 Hungarian Revolution, declared neutrality and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact, and was executed by the Soviet regime in 1958.

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Imre Nagy (1896–1958) was a lifelong Hungarian Communist politician who spent the World War II years in exile in Moscow (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Upon returning to Hungary in 1945, he served as Agriculture Minister, gaining wide popularity among the peasantry by implementing a major land reform program that distributed noble estates to poor families (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Premiership and Reforms (1953–1955)
Following [[joseph-stalin|Joseph Stalin]]'s death, the Soviet leadership forced the hardline Hungarian dictator [[matyas-rakosi|Matyas Rakosi]] to hand the premiership to Nagy (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Nagy introduced the "New Course," relaxing police terror, freeing political prisoners from camps, stopping forced collectivization, and producing more consumer goods (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). In 1955, [[matyas-rakosi|Rakosi]] maneuvered to have Nagy dismissed from office and expelled from the party (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Role in the 1956 Revolution
When the peaceful student march turned into an armed uprising on October 23, 1956, the public demanded the return of Nagy to the premiership (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Appointed Prime Minister on October 24, Nagy initially struggled to comprehend the scale of the crisis but soon sided with the popular demands (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). 

During the [[hungarian-revolution-1956|Twelve Days]], Nagy took several radical steps:
- **Abolition of the AVH**: He dissolved the hated secret police, the [[allamvedelmi-hatosag|AVH]] (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Multi-Party System**: He established a coalition government with non-communist parties (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
- **Declaration of Neutrality**: Upon discovering that new Soviet forces were invading, Nagy withdrew Hungary from the Warsaw Pact on November 1, declaring the country neutral and requesting United Nations protection (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Soviet Assault, Arrest, and Death
On November 4, 1956, when Soviet tanks invaded Budapest, Nagy made a final radio broadcast to the nation before seeking refuge at the Yugoslav Embassy (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Lured out with a promise of safe passage by Soviet puppet [[janos-kadar|Janos Kadar]], Nagy was arrested by Soviet security forces and deported to Romania (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

Nagy refused to confess or sign any document legitimizing the Soviet intervention (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Following a secret trial in Budapest, Nagy was sentenced to death and hanged on June 16, 1958 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). He was buried face down in an unmarked grave, only to be officially rehabilitated and reburied in June 1989 in a ceremony that signaled the end of Communist rule in Hungary (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
