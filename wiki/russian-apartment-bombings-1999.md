# 1999 Russian Apartment Bombings

**Summary**: A series of devastating apartment building explosions in September 1999 that killed nearly 300 Russians, triggered the Second Chechen War, and propelled Vladimir Putin to the presidency.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`, `Putin's_Playbook_-_Russia's_Secret_Plan_to_Defeat_America_9781684510030.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-16

---

The 1999 Russian Apartment Bombings (September 4–16, 1999) were a series of coordinate explosions targeting residential buildings in the Russian cities of Moscow, Buynaksk, and Volgodonsk. The attacks resulted in nearly 300 deaths, generated nationwide anti-Chechen panic, and served as the primary justification for launching the Second Chechen War.

## Timeline of the Attacks
* **September 4 (Buynaksk, Dagestan):** A car bomb detonated at a military housing complex, killing 64 people.
* **September 9 (Moscow):** A bomb split a nine-story apartment building at 19 Guryanova Street in two, killing 94 people (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
* **September 13 (Moscow):** An explosion demolished an apartment block on Kashirskoye Highway, killing 118 people (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
* **September 16 (Volgodonsk):** A truck bomb outside an apartment building killed 17 people (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## The Ryazan "Sugar" Incident
On September 22, 1999, residents of an apartment building in Ryazan reported suspicious activity involving three individuals carrying sacks into the basement from a white Lada. Local police found three sacks marked "sugar" containing a detonator and a timer set for 5:30 AM. A local bomb squad expert tested the sacks using a gas analyzer, identifying the substance as the military explosive hexogen (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

When the suspects were tracked via a Moscow telephone number, they were revealed to be FSB agents. On September 24, FSB Director Nikolai Patrushev announced that the Ryazan incident was merely a "training exercise" and that the sacks contained ordinary sugar. This explanation was met with widespread skepticism; local Ryazan authorities and bomb squad experts had no prior knowledge of the exercise and stood by their positive hexogen test (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Political Fallout and War
The bombings fueled intense anti-Chechen sentiment. Newly appointed Prime Minister [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] projected an image of decisive strength, famously promising to methodically hunt down the terrorists and *"waste them in the outhouse"* (*mochit' v sortire*) (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

On September 30, Russian forces launched a full-scale ground invasion of Chechnya. The war was widely popular in Russia and catapulted Putin's approval ratings, securing his victory in the March 2000 presidential election.

## False Flag Allegations and Crackdowns
Critics, including defected FSB officer Aleksandr Litvinenko and the independent newspaper *Novaya Gazeta*, alleged that the apartment bombings were a false flag operation carried out by the FSB itself to create a pretext for war and secure Putin's election (source: Putin's_Playbook_-_Russia's_Secret_Plan_to_Defeat_America_9781684510030.md; The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

The Russian government actively suppressed these claims:
- Litvinenko was poisoned and killed in London using radioactive Polonium-210 in 2006 (source: Putin's_Playbook_-_Russia's_Secret_Plan_to_Defeat_America_9781684510030.md).
- The independent television network NTV was taken over by state-backed Gazprom Media after broadcasting a documentary that questioned the government's role in the Ryazan incident (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
