# Washburn College

**Summary**: A non-sectarian educational institution in Topeka, Kansas, founded in 1865 as Lincoln College and renamed in 1868 to honor a major benefactor.

**Sources**: [Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Washburn College (originally Lincoln College) was conceived in April 1857 by the General Association of Congregational Ministers of Kansas to provide high-quality Christian education in the Territory (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). 

## Founding and Charter
The site selection was heavily contested between Lawrence and Topeka (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Though initially awarded to Lawrence, the 1860 drought delayed construction, and the college was relocated back to Topeka (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). The college was officially incorporated on February 6, 1865, as the "Trustees of Lincoln College" (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Reflecting its progressive roots, the charter explicitly mandated that education be provided "without distinction of color" (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## Relocation and Renaming
The first academy building was completed in December 1865 at Tenth and Jackson streets (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). In 1868, Deacon Ichabod Washburn of Worcester, Massachusetts, donated $25,000 to the endowment fund (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). In recognition of this gift, the trustees renamed the institution Washburn College (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).

## Expansion to College Hill
In 1871, Peter McVicar was elected president of the college and initiated a major expansion (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). The college sold its downtown building and hired prominent architect J. G. Haskell to construct a new collegiate building on the permanent 160-acre site donated by [[john-ritchie|John Ritchie]] (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md). Despite the financial depression of the mid-1870s, the $65,000 main building on "College Hill" was completed, and the college moved into its permanent campus in the fall of 1874 (source: Topeka pen and Camera Sketches.md).
