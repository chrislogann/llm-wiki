# Institutional Darwinism

**Summary**: A system of governance characterized by overlapping authorities and constant turf battles, where power goes to the strongest or most ruthless faction.

**Sources**: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Institutional Darwinism was a key feature of [[national-socialism]], as analyzed by Franz Neumann in [[behemoth]]. Instead of a strict vertical hierarchy, the Nazi state functioned as a "task state." 

Specific goals were entrusted to individuals with special authority, creating a system that cut across traditional bureaucratic domains (source: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md). This deliberate confusion and infighting were encouraged because they unleashed energies and fostered a "survival of the fittest" environment among the competing factions (like the party, the military, and big business).
