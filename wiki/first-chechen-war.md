# [[first-chechen-war|First Chechen War]]

**Summary**: The military conflict between the Russian Federation and the self-declared Chechen Republic of Ichkeria from December 1994 to August 1996, ending in a Chechen victory and Russian withdrawal.

**Sources**: `Chechnya_9780330350754.md`, `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The [[first-chechen-war|First Chechen War]] was a conflict triggered by [[chechnya|Chechnya]]'s unilateral declaration of independence under [[jokhar-dudayev|Jokhar Dudayev]] in 1991 following the collapse of the Soviet Union (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). Initially ignored by Moscow, the breakaway state was eventually invaded by Russian forces on December 11, 1994, under the direction of Russian President Boris Yeltsin (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

## Course of the Conflict

### The Battle for Grozny
The invasion began with a disastrous armored assault on Grozny on New Year's Eve 1994. Underestimating Chechen resistance, entire Russian armored columns were trapped and destroyed in the streets by mobile Chechen anti-tank squads (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). The Russian military responded by subjecting the city to weeks of heavy artillery bombardment and airstrikes, reducing Grozny to ruins and causing heavy civilian casualties (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### Mountain Guerilla Campaign
After the fall of Grozny in early 1995, Chechen fighters retreated to the southern mountains. Led by strategists like Chief of Staff [[aslan-maskhadov|Aslan Maskhadov]], they engaged in asymmetric, guerrilla warfare against the occupying Russian forces, turning the war into a bloody, unpopular stalemate for Moscow (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### Hostage Crises and Public Pressure
Faced with overwhelming conventional force, some Chechen commanders resorted to hostage-taking raids outside [[chechnya|Chechnya]]. In June 1995, field commander [[shamil-basayev|Shamil Basayev]] led a raid on Budyonnovsk, seizing a hospital and demanding a ceasefire (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). The raid forced Russian Prime Minister Viktor Chernomyrdin to negotiate a temporary truce, humiliating the Russian military and demonstrating the effectiveness of asymmetric tactics (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

### The August 1996 Offensive and Peace
In August 1996, Chechen fighters under [[aslan-maskhadov|Aslan Maskhadov]] launched a surprise counter-offensive, infiltrating Grozny and trapping thousands of Russian soldiers (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). Recognizing the military impossibility of holding the region, Russian Security Council Secretary General Alexander Lebed signed the **Khasavyurt Accord** with [[aslan-maskhadov|Maskhadov]] (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). The treaty led to the complete withdrawal of Russian troops and deferred the determination of [[chechnya|Chechnya]]'s official political status until 2001 (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md).

## Legacy
The war resulted in between 50,000 and 100,000 civilian deaths, devastated the region's economy, and deeply damaged the prestige of the post-Soviet Russian military (source: Chechnya_9780330350754.md). The unresolved political status and the rise of radicalized field commanders paved the way for the [[second-chechen-war|Second Chechen War]] in 1999 (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
