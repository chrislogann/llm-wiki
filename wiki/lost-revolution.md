# Lost Revolution - Germany 1918 To 1923

**Summary**: A summary of Chris Harman's history of the [[german-revolution|German Revolution]] (1918-1923), mapping out the key phases of working-class struggle and explaining how its defeat shaped the twin disasters of Stalinism and Nazism.

**Sources**: `Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md`, `Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

Chris Harman's *Lost Revolution* provides a detailed Marxist analysis of the [[german-revolution|German Revolution]] of 1918-1923, arguing that it was a genuine, non-inevitable revolutionary upheaval in the heart of an advanced Western industrial society (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).

## Key Themes

- **The Fall of the Monarchy & Workers' Councils**: The revolution began with the November 1918 sailors' revolt, which quickly led to the abdication of the Kaiser and the creation of workers' and soldiers' councils across Germany (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).
- **The Role of the Social Democrats (SPD)**: The SPD leadership allied with the old monarchist military High Command to restore state authority and systematically crush the revolutionary left, leading to the murder of Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).
- **The [[kapp-putsch|Kapp Putsch]]**: In March 1920, the right-wing military forces that the SPD had built up turned on the government in a coup. The putsch was defeated by a massive general strike and armed working-class resistance (the Red Ruhr Army) (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md).
- **Origins of Stalinism**: The failure of the German Revolution (the world's second industrial power) left the Russian Soviet state completely isolated, resulting in economic devastation, the collapse of its industrial proletariat, and the rise of Stalin's bureaucratic dictatorship (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). This isolation was cemented by the end of the [[russian-civil-war|Russian Civil War]], which left the Bolshevik regime victorious militarily but economically ruined and politically isolated (source: Russia_-_Revolution_and_Civil_War,_1917-1921_9780593493878.md).
- **Origins of Nazism**: The defeat of the revolutionary movement left the old military and capitalist structures intact. The [[swastika]] itself first entered modern German political conflict as the emblem worn on the helmets of the counter-revolutionary Freikorps during these battles (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). These intact structures (the military, the state bureaucracy, and big business) directly formed the foundation for the power centers described in Franz Neumann's analysis of the Nazi state; see the [[weimar-to-nazism-comparison]] page for more details.
