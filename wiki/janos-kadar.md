# Janos Kadar

**Summary**: The leader of Hungary from 1956 to 1988, who initially supported the 1956 Revolution before defecting to the Soviet side to form a puppet government that crushed the uprising and executed [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]].

**Sources**: `Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

Janos Kadar (1912–1989) was a working-class Hungarian Communist who served as Interior Minister under Stalinist dictator [[matyas-rakosi|Matyas Rakosi]] from 1948 to 1951, actively participating in the purge and trial of Laszlo Rajk (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). In 1951, Kadar himself was arrested, tortured by the secret police, and imprisoned until his release in 1954 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Role in the 1956 Revolution
When the [[hungarian-revolution-1956|1956 Revolution]] broke out, Kadar was appointed leader of the rebranded Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party and joined [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]]’s revolutionary cabinet (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). He initially gave speeches praising the "glorious uprising" of the workers and supported Nagy’s reforms (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

However, on November 1, as Nagy declared neutrality, Kadar secretly slipped out of Budapest and traveled to a Soviet military base (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Co-opted by Soviet leaders in Moscow, he agreed to form a pro-Soviet puppet government (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). On November 4, as Soviet tanks invaded, Kadar announced the formation of the "Revolutionary Worker-Peasant Government" to suppress the uprising (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

## Consolidation and "Goulash Communism"
Following the suppression of the revolution, Kadar oversaw a period of harsh reprisal. He reneged on safe-conduct promises for [[imre-nagy|Imre Nagy]], leading to Nagy's arrest, secret trial, and execution in 1958 (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). Over 20,000 Hungarians were imprisoned and hundreds executed under his early rule (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

Once his power was consolidated, Kadar shifted his strategy. In 1961, he famously declared: "He who is not against us is with us," introducing a series of economic reforms and cultural concessions known as **"Goulash Communism"** or **"Kadarism"** (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). Hungary became the "merriest barracks in the socialist camp," allowing small-scale private enterprise and travel opportunities in exchange for public compliance with political boundaries and loyalty to the Soviet Union (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).

Kadar ruled Hungary for 32 years, until he was forced to resign in May 1988 as the Soviet bloc was nearing collapse (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md). He died in July 1989, just as Hungary was dismantling the Iron Curtain (source: Twelve_Days_-_The_Story_of_the_1956_Hungarian_Revolution_9780375424588.md).
