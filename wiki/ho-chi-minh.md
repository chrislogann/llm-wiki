# [[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]]

**Summary**: The founder of the Indochinese Communist Party, leader of the Viet Minh independence movement, and President of North Vietnam, who led the struggle against French colonial rule and US intervention.

**Sources**: `Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

[[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]] (1890–1969), born Nguyen Sinh Cung and long known by the pseudonym Nguyen Ai Quoc ("Nguyen the Patriot"), was the primary leader of the Vietnamese nationalist and communist independence movement (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Early Life and [[comintern|Comintern]] Training
In 1911, Ho left Vietnam as a cook's helper on a French merchant ship, living in London and Paris (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). In Paris, he joined the French Socialist Party and attempted to present a petition for Vietnamese self-determination at the 1919 Versailles Peace Conference, which was ignored (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

Drawn to [[vladimir-lenin|Lenin]]’s anti-imperialist writings, Ho became a founding member of the French Communist Party in 1920 (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). He subsequently traveled to Moscow for training by the [[comintern|Comintern]], working as an agent in Canton, China, where he founded the Indochinese Communist Party in 1930 (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Founding of the Viet Minh and Independence
Ho returned to Vietnam in 1941 to establish the **Viet Minh** (League for the Independence of Vietnam), a broad nationalist coalition designed to fight the Japanese occupation and French colonial administration (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). In 1942, he adopted the name [[ho-chi-minh|Ho Chi Minh]] ("He Who Enlightens") (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

Following the Japanese surrender, Ho declared the independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV) in Hanoi on September 2, 1945, quoting the American Declaration of Independence (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Leadership in War
Ho led the nationalist forces through the **[[first-indochina-war|First Indochina War]]** against France, securing a partitioned North Vietnam in the **[[geneva-accords-1954|1954 Geneva Accords]]** (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). As President of the DRV, he implemented land reform and coordinated the insurgency in South Vietnam to unify the country (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 

Ho served as the moral and symbolic leader of the North Vietnamese struggle until his death in September 1969, six years before the final unification of Vietnam (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).
