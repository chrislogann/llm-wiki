## The "Silent Healer" on State TV
In the summer of 1989, 54-year-old former sports commentator [[allan-chumak]] rose to national prominence on Soviet Central Television (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md). His televised performances stood out due to their complete lack of sound:
* **Silent Gestures**: Chumak sat before the camera noiselessly making repetitive hand movements, moving his lips without speaking, and occasionally sighing to push back force fields and sweep away bad energy (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **"Charging" Items**: Millions of viewers placed jars of water and tubs of skin cream close to their television screens, believing Chumak's gestures would "charge" the liquids with healing powers to remedy medical conditions ranging from allergies to paralysis (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).

## Societal Drivers under Perestroika
The article identifies several key cultural conditions that allowed Chumak to dominate the public consciousness:
* **Healthcare and Economic Decline**: The collapse of faith in Soviet atheist materialism matched a rapid decline in the state healthcare system. While ostensibly free, healthcare suffered from severe shortages of modern drugs, medical equipment, and basic goods, leading citizens to seek alternatives (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **Glasnost and Trust in State Media**: Media liberalization under Glasnost allowed state channels to broadcast non-traditional programs, while the stuffiness and authority of state television lent institutional legitimacy to Chumak's claims (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **Vague Philosophy**: Chumak's cosmic-spiritual philosophy, which he claimed to receive through an "inner voice" at age 42, was intentionally non-specific, promising cure-alls without committing to concrete claims (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).

## Feud with Kashpirovsky and Banishment
* **Contrast in Styles**: While Chumak was perceived as a kindly, non-threatening "white mage," his chief rival [[anatoly-kashpirovsky]] possessed a "demonic," black-clad, aggressive presence, growling at illnesses and causing patients to collapse on stage (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **Feud**: Though initially friends, the two engaged in a bitter, decades-long feud over who had appeared on television first (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
* **1993 Ban**: Chumak successfully monetized his TV career by mass-producing pre-charged water and creams (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md). In 1993, the Ministry of Health officially banned television psychics for "advocating pseudoscience" (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md). Chumak spent his remaining years charging high fees for private healing sessions and performing in smaller venues (source: Soviet Union’s mass delusion Psychic healer who ‘cured’ millions through TV dies.md).
