# Cimarron County, Oklahoma

**Summary**: The westernmost county in the Oklahoma Panhandle, which was at the geographic heart of the 1930s Dust Bowl and experienced severe soil erosion and a catastrophic agricultural collapse.

**Sources**: [Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md]

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

Cimarron County, Oklahoma, particularly its county seat Boise City, was identified by government photographers and experts as the absolute center of the Dust Bowl's severe wind erosion (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md).

## Mechanized Sodbusting
In the late 1920s, Cimarron County was heavily plowed up in a rapid, mechanized expansion (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). Local dealers like Julius Cox sold dozens of tractors to sodbusters eager to plant wheat (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). This massive removal of native grasslands left the sandy topsoil vulnerable to the high winds when drought arrived in the early 1930s.

## The Cattle Crisis
Ranchers in Cimarron County suffered severe losses as grazing lands dried up (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). Overgrazing compounded the drought, and by the mid-1930s, starving cattle were reduced to eating tumbleweeds (Russian thistle) (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md). The federal government eventually stepped in to purchase and slaughter dying cattle as a relief measure, with most surviving animals shipped out of the county (source: Dust_Bowl_-_the_southern_Plains_in_the_1930s_9780195174885.md).
