# California Agriculture

**Summary**: The agricultural systems and economic landscape of the California Central Valley, marked by industrial scale, land centralization, and intense labor struggles.

**Sources**: [The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md](file:///home/chrislogann/Desktop/repository/llm-wiki/raw/The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-09

---

California's agricultural industry, centered in the fertile Central Valley, was historically dominated by large landholdings and corporate interests rather than small family farms (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md). The consolidation of land ownership allowed growers to dictate wages and working conditions across the state (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).

## Industrial Consolidation
In the 1930s, farming transitioned into an industrial business. Large growers and utility corporations owned the canneries and vast tracts of land, squeezing out smaller farmers who could not compete with industrialized efficiency or secure credit from banks (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).

## Associated Farmers and Wage Suppression
Organizations like the Associated Farmers of California worked collectively to fix low wage rates for harvest labor (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md). They used political influence, local deputies, and armed vigilantes to suppress strikes, blacklist union organizers, and close down roadside camps to force workers into company-owned quarters (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).

This system of agricultural exploitation and the resulting class friction is a core focus of [[the-grapes-of-wrath]] (source: The_Grapes_of_Wrath_9780143039433.md).
