# Ogaden War

**Summary**: The 1977–1978 conflict between Ethiopia and Somalia over the Ogaden region, featuring a massive Soviet-Cuban military intervention that successfully repelled the Somali invasion but effectively destroyed superpower *détente*.

**Sources**: `Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The Ogaden War began in July 1977 when the Somali Democratic Republic, led by Siad Barre, invaded the Ogaden region in eastern Ethiopia (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Barre sought to capitalize on the instability of the **[[ethiopian-revolution]]** to annex the territory, which was populated primarily by ethnic Somalis (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Superpower Realignment
Before the war, both Ethiopia and Somalia had ties to the superpowers: Ethiopia was traditionally backed by the United States, while Somalia was a Soviet client state (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). The war forced a dramatic reversal:
- Siad Barre expelled Soviet advisors and terminated his alliance with Moscow, hoping to receive military aid from the United States and Saudi Arabia (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
- The Soviet Union and Cuba chose to support Mengistu Haile Mariam's Marxist regime in Addis Ababa, viewing Ethiopia's revolution as more genuine and strategically vital than Somalia's (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## The Soviet-Cuban Intervention
Between November 1977 and March 1978, the Soviet Union executed a massive military air bridge, delivering approximately $1 billion in advanced weapons and sending military advisors to coordinate the Ethiopian defense (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). 

Simultaneously, Cuba deployed over 15,000 combat troops to the frontline (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Led by Cuban and Soviet commanders, these forces launched a decisive counteroffensive that shattered the Somali army and forced a full Somali withdrawal by March 1978 (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Consequences

### 1. Collapse of Somalia
The military defeat undermined Siad Barre's regime (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). To maintain power, Barre imposed heavy taxes on provinces, reigniting clan rivalries and eventually plunging Somalia into a devastating civil war and state collapse in the late 1980s (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 2. Death of Détente
The scale and success of the Soviet-Cuban intervention alarmed the Carter administration in the United States (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). It convinced U.S. policymakers that Moscow was utilizing military proxy force to expand its **[[peripheral-empire]]**, effectively bringing the period of superpower *détente* to an end and setting the stage for the confrontational policies of the 1980s (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
