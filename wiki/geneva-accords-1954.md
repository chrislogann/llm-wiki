# [[geneva-accords-1954|Geneva Accords]] 1954

**Summary**: The 1954 peace agreement that ended the [[first-indochina-war|First Indochina War]], temporarily partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel, established the independence of Laos and Cambodia, and scheduled nationwide elections for 1956.

**Sources**: `Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **[[geneva-accords-1954|Geneva Accords]]** of 1954 were the results of the Geneva Conference, convened in Switzerland from April to July 1954 to resolve the conflicts in Indochina and Korea (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). The conference included representatives from France, the Viet Minh (DRV), the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, the People’s Republic of China, the United States, and the State of Vietnam (South Vietnam) (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).

## Key Provisions
Following the French defeat at the **[[battle-of-dien-bien-phu|Battle of Dien Bien Phu]]**, the negotiators agreed to the following terms (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md):
- **Temporary Partition**: Vietnam was temporarily divided at the **17th parallel** into two zones: the communist Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV) in the north, and the French-backed State of Vietnam in the south.
- **Demilitarized Zone (DMZ)**: A five-mile-wide demilitarized zone was established along the partition line to separate the opposing militaries.
- **National Elections**: Free nationwide elections were scheduled for July 1956 to unify the country under a single government.
- **Foreign Military Bans**: Both zones were prohibited from entering military alliances or hosting foreign military bases.
- **Independence of Neighbors**: The independence and neutrality of Laos and Cambodia were formally recognized, ending French colonial control over all of Indochina.

## Geopolitical Discrepancies and Rejection
The Accords were signed by France and the DRV, but were not signed by the United States or the State of Vietnam (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). 
- **The DRV Compromise**: Under pressure from their Soviet and Chinese sponsors (who wanted to avoid a direct military confrontation with the US), the Viet Minh accepted the temporary partition, despite controlling the vast majority of Vietnamese territory.
- **The US Stance**: The Eisenhower administration issued a unilateral declaration stating that the US would refrain from using force to disturb the accords but would view any renewal of aggression with grave concern.
- **Cancellation of Elections**: In 1956, South Vietnamese President **[[ngo-dinh-diem|Ngo Dinh Diem]]**, with US backing, refused to hold the scheduled nationwide elections, arguing that South Vietnam had not signed the accords and that free elections were impossible in the communist North (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md). This failure to hold elections solidified the division of the country and paved the way for the Vietnam War (source: Vietnam,_a_history_9780670746040.md).
