# Weimar to Nazism: Comparative Analysis

**Summary**: A comparative analysis of Chris Harman's *Lost Revolution* (1918-1923) and Franz Neumann's *Behemoth* (1933-1944), detailing how the survival of Germany's traditional elites directly enabled the structure of the Nazi state.

**Sources**: `Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md`, `Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

By examining Chris Harman's [[lost-revolution]] alongside Franz Neumann's [[behemoth]], a continuous historical narrative emerges explaining both the survival and eventual configuration of Germany's ruling class during the transition from the Weimar Republic to the Third Reich. 

## The Continuity of Elites

The core connection between the two works lies in the **preservation of traditional structures**:
- **The Weimar Birth (1918-1923)**: Harman details how the Social Democratic Party (SPD) leadership allied with the monarchist military High Command to systematically suppress the revolutionary left (source: Lost_Revolution_-_Germany_1918_To_1923_9781608463169.md). This decision left the old capitalist ownership structures and state bureaucracy completely untouched.
- **The Nazi State (1933-1944)**: Neumann's analysis of the Nazi state structure reveals that these very same preserved elites—the **state bureaucracy**, the **armed forces**, and **big business**—became three of the four key power centers (the "quadrumvirate") that ruled the Third Reich (source: Behemoth_-_the_structure_and_practice_of_national_socialism,_1933-1944_9781566638197.md).

Without the SPD's preservation of the old order during the revolutionary period described by Harman, the institutional power base that Neumann details in *Behemoth* would not have survived to align itself with the Nazi Party.

## Theoretical Methodologies

Both authors apply a [[marxist-analysis]] framework, but their focus areas differ:
- **Harman** utilizes a classic Marxist historical-materialist approach, focusing on class struggle, the role of workers' councils, and how the political actions of political organizations (SPD, KPD) determined the failure of the revolution.
- **Neumann** implements a structuralist Marxist critique of the fascist state. He focuses on the legal, administrative, and economic mechanisms of the Nazi regime, arguing that fascism is an extreme form of monopolistic capitalism where the state and big business co-opt each other.

## Structural Evolution: From Republic to Behemoth

| Feature | *Lost Revolution* (Harman) | *Behemoth* (Neumann) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Historical Period** | 1918-1923 (Weimar Crisis) | 1933-1944 (Third Reich) |
| **State Structure** | Weak democratic republic under threat from both left and right | A chaotic, lawless "monster" or "task state" with overlapping jurisdictions |
| **Role of Big Business** | Preserved by Weimar authorities; funded counter-revolutionary Freikorps | Co-opted by and symbiotic with the Nazi state; concentrated monopoly capital |
| **Role of Military** | Collaborated with the SPD to crush workers' councils; attempted coups like [[kapp-putsch]] | Formed one of the four symbiotic power centers of the regime |

Ultimately, *Lost Revolution* explains the *causes* of the preservation of Germany's reactionary forces, while *Behemoth* explains the *consequences* of that survival once those forces consolidated their power under fascism.
