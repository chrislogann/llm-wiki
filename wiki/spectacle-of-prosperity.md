# Spectacle of Prosperity

**Summary**: The use of advertising, public relations, and marketing in 1920s America to manipulate consumer demand, representing non-terrorist fascist processes designed to sustain capital accumulation.

**Sources**: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **spectacle of prosperity** refers to the material abundance and rapid economic growth in the United States between 1922 and 1929. Behind this dazzle of consumer goods, automobiles, and home electrification lay a critical capitalist contradiction: productivity rose far faster than wages, threating a crisis of overproduction unless consumption could be artificially expanded (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).

## Marketing as a Fascist Process

- **Non-Terrorist Fascist Processes**: Drawing on Herbert Marcuse's concept of a non-terrorist totalitarian coordination of society, Michael Joseph Roberto argues that marketing in the 1920s functioned as a fascist process. It extended the domination of capital into the realm of mass consciousness, substituting active citizenship with passive consumerism (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Rise of the Salesman**: Marketing and salesmanship became highly psychological industries designed to "break down consumer resistance." The salesman (exemplified by Sinclair Lewis's literary character George F. Babbitt) had to sell his own personality to sell commodities, aligning his self-identity entirely with the market's demands (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Deification of Business**: Business was elevated to a civic religion. In his 1925 bestseller *The Man Nobody Knows*, advertising executive Bruce Barton famously reinterpreted Jesus Christ as the founder of modern business and the first modern executive who built a successful sales organization, integrating business philosophy directly into Christian culture (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Advertising Boom**: Between 1914 and 1929, national advertising expenditures quadrupled, rising to $1.1 billion. This massive effort was structural, aiming to prevent the vast output of mechanized industry from being dammed at its outlet (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
