# Three Goals of Intelligence Writing

**Summary**: The core objectives that define the intelligence analyst's output: making judgments about the future, interpreting foreign cultures and alien problems, and supporting decision makers.

**Sources**: [Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md](file:///home/chrislogann/Desktop/repository/llm-wiki/raw/Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md)

**Last updated**: 2026-08-15

---

According to *Communicating with Intelligence*, transitioning from an academic to an analytical mind-set requires aligning one's writing with three primary goals (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).

## Goal 1: Make Judgments about the Future
Unlike historians who focus on what happened, intelligence analysts focus on what events *mean for the future*.
- **Incomplete Information**: Analysts must make judgments based on conflicting and incomplete data.
- **No Luxury of Time**: Analysts cannot delay products to wait for perfect information; they must provide the best answer given the time available.
- **Probabilistic Mindset**: Analysts must accept that they will sometimes be wrong, but refraining from predicting is a failure of their mission.

## Goal 2: Interpret Foreign Cultures and Alien Problems
Analysts act as cultural interpreters, making sense of foreign developments for domestic policy makers.
- **Avoiding Mirror-Imaging**: What seems irrational in one's own culture may be entirely logical from a foreign perspective. Culture, religion, history, and ideology govern local behavior.
- **Historical Failures**: The book cites historical failures resulting from cultural ignorance:
  - **Vietnam War**: CIA director Richard Helms acknowledged a national ignorance of Vietnamese history and society (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).
  - **Afghan War (2001–2021)**: White House Afghan war czar Douglas Lute noted a failure to accumulate deep understanding of Afghanistan, leading to the rapid collapse of the national government in 2021 (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).

## Goal 3: Support Decision Makers
Intelligence serves the policy process but does not direct it.
- **Political Agnosticism**: Analysts are forbidden from making policy recommendations. As Sherman Kent emphasized, intelligence is an ancillary "service function" (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).
- **Telling Truth to Power**: Analysts must present objective findings, even if they are unwelcome.
- **Hayden Iraq Briefing (2007)**: In 2007, CIA Director Michael Hayden briefed President George W. Bush that the situation in Iraq was "very bad" (describing it as a civil war and "one of Dante's circles of hell"). This objective, albeit unwelcome intelligence, directly influenced the president's decision to authorize the troop surge (source: Communicating_with_Intelligence_9781538160688.md).
