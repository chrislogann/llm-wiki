# Boris Yeltsin

**Summary**: The first President of the Russian Federation who dissolved the Soviet Union, initiated shock therapy, and navigated major constitutional crises.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-16

---

Boris Yeltsin (1931–2007) was a Soviet and Russian politician who served as the first President of the Russian Federation (1991–1999). He was a key figure in the collapse of the Soviet Union and overseen Russia's transition to a market economy, a process marked by severe economic hardship and constitutional conflicts.

## Conflict with Gorbachev and Rise to Power
Yeltsin gained prominence as a reformer within the Communist Party under Mikhail Gorbachev, serving as the first secretary of the Moscow party committee. However, his direct and outspoken criticisms of the party's leadership during the October 1987 plenum led to his expulsion from the Politburo and political ostracization, orchestrated by Gorbachev and the KGB (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

Despite campaigns to discredit him, Yeltsin returned to prominence on May 29, 1990, when he was elected Chairman of the Russian Supreme Soviet (effectively President of the Russian Federation). He championed Russian sovereignty, asserting that Russian laws took precedence over Soviet ones (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## August 1991 Coup and the Dissolution of the USSR
During the [[august-coup-1991|August 1991 coup attempt]] by hardline Soviet officials, Yeltsin led the democratic resistance from the Moscow White House, standing as a symbol of opposition to the putsch. He worked closely with other reformist leaders, including St. Petersburg Mayor [[anatoly-sobchak|Anatoly Sobchak]] (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

Following the coup's collapse, Yeltsin banned the Communist Party, seized its assets, and ultimately signed the accords that dissolved the Soviet Union in December 1991 (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## The [[russian-constitutional-crisis-1993|1993 Constitutional Crisis]]
Yeltsin's transition to a capitalist economy, known as "shock therapy," caused extreme inflation and impoverished millions, leading to fierce gridlock between the president and the parliament. In September 1993, Yeltsin unconstitutionally dissolved the Supreme Soviet and the Congress of People's Deputies. In the ensuing violent standoff, Yeltsin ordered military tanks to shell the White House building, leading to over 100 deaths and consolidating a highly centralized executive presidency (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## The 1996 Re-election and the Oligarchic Alliance
By late 1995, Yeltsin's popularity dropped to 3% due to economic collapse and the unpopular First Chechen War. Fearing a communist return, Russia's wealthiest bankers and media moguls formed the "Davos Pact" to bankroll his 1996 re-election campaign. Under campaign manager Anatoly Chubais, they spent up to $2 billion and used corporate media dominance to secure Yeltsin's victory (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Handover of Power to Putin and Legacy
Failing health and corruption investigations prompted Yeltsin to search for a successor from the security services. His administration promoted [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to head the FSB and then appointed him Prime Minister.

On December 31, 1999, Yeltsin announced his sudden resignation on national television, making Putin the Acting President. Yeltsin's resignation secured absolute immunity from prosecution for himself and his family (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
