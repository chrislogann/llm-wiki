# Comparison: Propaganda Techniques and the Law of Advertising

**Summary**: A comparison and synthesis of the Nazi propaganda techniques (analyzed in Willi Münzenberg's *Propaganda als Waffe*) and the John Blair "Law of Advertising" under American monopoly capitalism (analyzed in Michael Joseph Roberto's *Coming of the American Behemoth*).

While one operates in a liberal-democratic commercial marketplace and the other within a totalitarian state, both the **Law of Advertising** and **Nazi propaganda techniques** share a structural reliance on psychological saturation, elite manipulation of public opinion, and the neutralization of class conflict.

## 1. Blair's Law of Advertising: The Saturation Feedback Loop

In the American business system, economist John Blair (1938) identified a self-reinforcing loop governing mass consumption:
* **The Psychological Shell**: Continuous exposure to mass marketing causes consumers to build a defensive, jaded mental barrier against advertisements.
* **Escalating Intensity**: To penetrate this shell and prevent industrial overproduction, advertisers must deploy increasingly intensive, emotionally manipulative, and psychologically sophisticated campaigns.
* **The Loop**: This escalation hardens the shell further, requiring even more aggressive advertising *ad infinitum*, mirroring the general law of capitalist accumulation.

## 2. Nazi Propaganda Techniques: Reklame as a Weapon

Willi Münzenberg's *Propaganda als Waffe* shows how Goebbels and Hitler adapted these commercial mass-marketing (*Reklame*) methods to state control:
* **Will to Power over Minds**: Eugen Hadamovsky defined propaganda as the "will to power... to secure power over minds." Rather than educating, it seeks to subjugate critical thinking, treating the masses as unformed "raw material" to be shaped by the "state artist."
* **Simplification and Instinct**: Nazi propaganda rejects objectivity and intellectual evaluation. It reduces complex realities to binary slogans and appeals to primitive instincts (brutality, fear, and chauvinism) to keep the public mobilization high.
* **Plagiarizing Commercial Success**: Hitler and Goebbels openly studied and copied Allied WWI propaganda and corporate marketing, praising their absolute lack of objectivity and radical tendentiousness.

## 3. Parallel Methods of Social Control

Both systems recognized the necessity of managing mass consciousness to maintain class relations and centralized control, substituting material reforms with psychological illusions:

* **The Illusion of Equality**:
  * **American Advertising**: The *Democracy of Goods* parable (Roland Marchand) convinced the public that buying the same products as Park Avenue millionaires made them equal, deflecting demands for actual wealth redistribution.
  * **Nazi Germany**: The concept of *Volksgemeinschaft* (people's community) and Ley's *Kraft durch Freude* (Strength through Joy) programs convinced workers that aesthetic participation (like state-sponsored travel) replaced the need for structural economic reforms.
* **The Invisible Government**:
  * **American Advertising**: Edward Bernays and Harold Lasswell mapped out systems for "invisible wirepullers" to manipulate public habits and opinions, turning public opinion into a commodity.
  * **Nazi Germany**: The Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda exercised totalizing, overt control over the press, schools, and cultural institutions to ensure complete ideological coordination (*Gleichschaltung*).
