# Vertical of Power

**Summary**: The governance system established by Russian President [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] to centralize state authority, strip regional autonomy, subdue oligarchs, and bring the national media under state control.

**Sources**: `The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md`, `Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The **"vertical of power"** (*vertikal vlasti*) is the term used to describe the centralization of political authority in the Russian Federation under [[vladimir-putin|Vladimir Putin]] (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Designed as a corrective to the weak central government and regional fragmentation of the Boris Yeltsin era in the 1990s, the system concentrated decision-making power within the Kremlin (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

## Core Mechanisms

### 1. Regional Consolidation
Upon taking office in 2000, Putin divided Russia's 89 regions into seven federal districts, placing each under the control of a presidential envoy, many of whom had military or security backgrounds (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Following the 2004 Beslan school siege, Putin abolished direct elections for regional governors, replacing them with a system where the Russian president nominates governors for approval by local legislatures (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

### 2. Media Control
The Kremlin systematically targeted independent media outlets. In 2001, the state-controlled gas giant Gazprom seized control of NTV, Russia's premier independent television network, which had run critical coverage of the [[second-chechen-war|Second Chechen War]] and parodied Putin (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). Similar campaigns brought other major television channels, radio stations, and print publications under state management or the ownership of loyalist oligarchs (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

### 3. Neutralizing the Oligarchs
Putin offered Russia's wealthy business elites a deal: they could retain their wealth and assets as long as they stayed out of politics and supported the Kremlin's policies. Those who resisted, such as media tycoon Boris Berezovsky and oil billionaire Mikhail Khodorkovsky (head of Yukos), were prosecuted, exiled, or imprisoned, and their corporate assets were absorbed by the state or transferred to Putin's allies (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md). This created a system of state capitalism colloquially known as "Kremlin, Inc." (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

### 4. Parliamentary Subjugation
Through the creation and consolidation of the United Russia party (*Yedinaya Rossiya*), the Kremlin secured a constitutional supermajority in the State Duma. This turned the legislature into a body that rubber-stamps presidential initiatives (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).

### 5. Domestic Security Control
The physical preservation of the vertical of power is guaranteed by Russia's domestic **[[internal-security-forces]]**, including the FSB and Rosgvardiya (National Guard) (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md). Subordinate directly to the presidency, these security structures serve as the ultimate checkpoint to suppress political opposition, counter civil unrest, and prevent foreign-backed "color revolutions" (source: Russia's_military_revival_9781509516148.md).

## Impact
The vertical of power succeeded in stabilizing the Russian state after the chaos of the 1990s and restored economic growth, but it did so by dismantling democratic institutions, establishing an authoritarian state, and cultivating systemic corruption (source: The_New_Tsar_-_the_rise_and_reign_of_Vladimir_Putin_9780307961617.md).
