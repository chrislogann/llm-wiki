# Reagan Doctrine

**Summary**: The U.S. foreign policy strategy in the 1980s under President Ronald Reagan designed to roll back Soviet influence in the Third World by providing overt and covert support to anti-communist insurgencies.

**Sources**: `Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md`, `The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

The Reagan Doctrine (often analyzed as the "Reagan offensive" in global history) represented a shift in U.S. Cold War strategy from containing communism to active rollback (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Driven by a revitalized **[[empire-of-liberty]]** ideology, the Reagan administration aimed to exploit the vulnerabilities of the Soviet Union's **[[peripheral-empire]]** (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md, source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Core Strategy
The doctrine targeted newly established, fragile Marxist-Leninist regimes in the Third World that relied on Soviet or Cuban support (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Rather than engaging in direct military interventions, the United States funded, armed, and trained local anti-communist guerrilla movements (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

## Key Theatres of Intervention

### 1. Afghanistan
The United States, in partnership with Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, dramatically increased covert aid to the Islamist Mujahideen resisting the Soviet occupation in the **[[soviet-afghan-war]]** (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). Providing advanced weaponry, including Stinger surface-to-air missiles, helped neutralize Soviet air superiority and created a military quagmire for Moscow (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 2. Angola
The U.S. repealed the Clark Amendment (which had banned aid to Angolan factions) to provide overt and covert backing to Jonas Savimbi's UNITA in the **[[angolan-civil-war]]**, opposing the Soviet-backed MPLA government and Cuban combat forces (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 3. Nicaragua
Following the 1979 Sandinista revolution, the CIA organized, funded, and armed the counter-revolutionary "Contras" to destabilize the leftist Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN) government (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).

### 4. Grenada
In October 1983, the U.S. launched a direct military invasion of Grenada, overthrowing the Marxist military council that had seized power (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md). This marked the first physical rollback of a Soviet client regime (source: The_rise_and_fall_of_the_Soviet_Empire_9780761520573.md).

## Impact and Legacy
The Reagan Doctrine placed severe financial and military strain on the Soviet Union, contributing to Mikhail Gorbachev's decision to withdraw Soviet forces from regional proxy wars (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md). However, the strategy also left a legacy of devastation, civil conflict, and highly armed extremist groups in the targeted nations (source: Global_Cold_War_-_Third_World_Interventions_and_the_Making_of_Our_Times_9780511817991.md).
