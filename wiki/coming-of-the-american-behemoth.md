# Coming of the American Behemoth

**Summary**: A historical analysis by Michael Joseph Roberto detailing the origins of fascism in the United States between 1920 and 1940, tracing it to the growth of monopoly-finance capital and the authoritarian tendencies within the business system.

**Sources**: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

*Coming of the American Behemoth* explores how the seeds of American fascism were sown during the prosperous 1920s and grew into a distinct political force during the Great Depression of the 1930s. Roberto recovers a "lost" discourse from 1930s and 1940s writers who argued that the true source of American fascism was not fringe mass movements or populist demagogues, but Big Business, monopoly-finance capital, and Wall Street (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).

## Key Themes

- **Fascism as the Dictatorship of Capital**: Roberto rejects the sterile paradigm of defining fascism solely by European characteristics (like para-military formations, one-party rule, or a charismatic dictator). Instead, he defines fascism fundamentally as the [[dictatorship-of-capital]] over society.
- **The Germ of Fascism in the 1920s**: The book argues that fascist processes are inherent in capitalist accumulation and modernization, which naturally centralize economic power and intensify the exploitation and displacement of labor.
- **The Spectacle of Prosperity**: During the 1920s, capitalists generated a [[spectacle-of-prosperity]] that relied on advertising, public relations, and consumerism to sustain the high rate of consumption necessary for capital accumulation, representing non-terrorist fascist processes.
- **Fascism in the Business System**: Drawing on the economist Robert A. Brady, the book details how the highly organized [[business-system-and-fascism]] became a totalizing power, utilizing trade organizations like the National Association of Manufacturers (NAM) to coordinate political power and create a corporatist structure.
- **The Role of the New Deal**: Roberto recovers the historical debate on whether the New Deal was a transition to state monopoly capitalism resembling Italian corporatism, and examines FDR's critique of monopoly power, highlighting the [[good-vs-bad-capitalism]] false dichotomy.
