# Coming of the American Behemoth

**Summary**: A historical analysis by Michael Joseph Roberto detailing the origins of fascism in the United States between 1920 and 1940, tracing it to the growth of monopoly-finance capital and the authoritarian tendencies within the business system.

**Sources**: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md

**Last updated**: 2026-08-11

---

*Coming of the American Behemoth* explores how the seeds of American fascism were sown during the prosperous 1920s and grew into a distinct political force during the Great Depression of the 1930s. Roberto recovers a "lost" discourse from 1930s and 1940s writers who argued that the true source of American fascism was not fringe mass movements or populist demagogues, but Big Business, monopoly-finance capital, and Wall Street (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).

## Key Themes

- **Fascism as the Dictatorship of Capital**: Roberto rejects the sterile paradigm of defining fascism solely by European characteristics (like para-military formations, one-party rule, or a charismatic dictator). Instead, he defines fascism fundamentally as the [[dictatorship-of-capital]] over society (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Germ of Fascism in the 1920s**: The book argues that fascist processes are inherent in capitalist accumulation and modernization, which naturally centralize economic power and intensify the exploitation and displacement of labor (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Spectacle of Prosperity and Advertisement**: During the 1920s, capitalists generated a [[spectacle-of-prosperity]] that relied on advertising and public relations as non-terrorist fascist processes. Key aspects include:
  * **Silas Bent's Critique**: Journalist Silas Bent noted that advertising enabled extreme markups (e.g. selling a penny's worth of antiseptic for $95) and that the packaging of news by centralized media companies mirrored the commercial methods used to sell commodities (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
  * **Manufacturing Consent**: Elite public opinion experts like Walter Lippmann argued that a small ruling elite had to "manufacture consent" for a public incapable of understanding the world clearly, shaping media to captivate and sensationalize (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
  * **The Fusing of Business and Religion**: Bruce Barton's deification of corporate leadership in *The Man Nobody Knows* (1925) reframed Jesus as an advertising executive and his parables as marketing tools (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
  * **The Democracy of Goods**: Roland Marchand's analysis showed how advertisers used parables of consumption to convince the public that buying the same products as Park Avenue millionaires made them equal, deflecting class conflict and demands for wealth redistribution (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **Fascism in the Business System**: Drawing on the economist Robert A. Brady, the book details how the highly organized [[business-system-and-fascism]] became a totalizing power, utilizing trade organizations like the National Association of Manufacturers (NAM) to coordinate political power and create a corporatist structure (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
- **The Role of the New Deal**: Roberto recovers the historical debate on whether the New Deal was a transition to state monopoly capitalism resembling Italian corporatism, and examines FDR's critique of monopoly power, highlighting the [[good-vs-bad-capitalism]] false dichotomy (source: Coming_of_the_American_Behemoth_-_The_Origins_of_Fascism_in_the_United_States,_1920_-1940_9781583677315.md).
