Computational thinking is a critical problem-solving technique in computer science that allows individuals to address complex, non-obvious problems by breaking them down into manageable pieces and planning solutions that computers or humans can implement (source: What is computational thinking - Introduction to computational thinking - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).

## The Four Cornerstones

There are four key techniques (cornerstones) to computational thinking (source: What is computational thinking - Introduction to computational thinking - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md):
- **Decomposition**: Breaking a complex problem or system down into smaller, more manageable parts.
- **Pattern Recognition**: Finding similarities or patterns among and within problems.
- **Abstraction**: Filtering out specific characteristics or details that are not needed to focus on the essential aspects.
- **Algorithms**: Developing step-by-step instructions or rules to solve the problem.

Each of these cornerstones is crucial. Missing even one component makes it highly difficult to build a functional, correct computer program (source: What is computational thinking - Introduction to computational thinking - KS3 Computer Science Revision - BBC Bitesize.md).
