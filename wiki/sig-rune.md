# Sig Rune

**Summary**: An ancient runic symbol representing the letter "S" that was modified and renamed by the SS to symbolize "victory," leading to its modern association with Nazism.

**Sources**: `Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-08-04

---

The sig rune originated in the Viking Age as a character in runic inscriptions representing the letter "S" (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). 

## Nazi Redesign and Misappropriation

- **Walter Heck**: In 1931, graphic designer and SS-Sturmführer Walter Heck drew two sig-runes side by side while working for a badge-manufacturing company (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). 
- **Purchase and Renaming**: The SS purchased the rights to Heck's double-rune design for 2.50 Reichsmarks and renamed it the *siegrune* (victory rune) (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md).
- **Stigma and Legal Ban**: The double sig-rune became the primary insignia of the SS, forging an unbreakable association with Nazi atrocities (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md). Along with the swastika, the symbol is legally banned in Germany under Section 86a of the Criminal Code (source: Nazism and Nordic Mythology.md) and remains heavily stigmatized in modern scholarship.
